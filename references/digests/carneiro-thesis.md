## One-paragraph summary

The thesis gives a from-scratch, self-contained axiomatic presentation of Lean's underlying dependent type theory (universes with `imax`, Pi/forall, inductive types with a general specification calculus, quotients, propositional extensionality, and choice), then spends most of its length proving three things about that axiomatic system: (1) unique typing (Section/`unique.tex`, via a delicate `\vdash_n`-indexed induction that simultaneously builds a Church-Rosser theorem for a restricted "kappa" reduction and a "proof irrelevance" congruence, because ordinary Church-Rosser fails outright once proof irrelevance is added); (2) that the entire zoo of inductive types is inter-derivable with eight primitive type formers plus $\mathsf{W}$-types (`Wtypes.tex`), used later to make the soundness proof tractable; and (3) a set-theoretic soundness interpretation of (a "proof-irrelevance-split" variant of) the type theory into ZFC augmented with countably many inaccessible cardinals (`soundness.tex`), yielding Lean's consistency as a corollary. Two final chapters are left incomplete in this source: `normalization.tex` sketches (but does not finish) a terminating restriction of the kappa-reduction relation suitable for an actual normalization/decision procedure, and `compilation.tex` sketches (in two rule blocks, no accompanying prose) an **erasure-style compilation** of the type theory into a two-sorted target calculus with types `\ast` (erased) and `\obj` (retained data) plus function types, which is the closest point of contact in this document to the untyped λ□ compilation target used downstream.

## What it establishes

- Lean's typing judgment, definitional equality (`≡`, ideal) and algorithmic equality (`⇔`, what the checker uses) as separate relations, plus the reduction relation `⇝`, presented from scratch as inference rules -> `axioms.tex` §"Typing"/"Definitional equality"/"Reduction", full page.
- Universe levels (`0`, `S`, `max`, `imax`) with an inequality judgment `ℓ ≤ ℓ'+n` and its semantics in `\N` -> `axioms.tex` §"Typing" end (level semantics paragraph), and used throughout.
- `let` (`ζ`), constant definitions (`δ`), and a general inductive-type specification calculus (`spec`/`ctor` judgments), the recursor's type, and the `ι` computation rule including "K-like reduction" for subsingleton eliminators -> `axioms.tex` §"let binders", §"Definitions", §"Inductive types" (`sec:inductive`), §"The recursor", §"The computation rule" (`sec:iota`).
- Large elimination and subsingleton (SS) elimination as the two reasons an inductive family can be large-eliminating, with an explicit `LE`/`LEctor` judgment -> `axioms.tex` §"Large elimination" (`sec:large_elim`).
- Non-primitive axioms: quotient types (with `mk`, `sound`, `lift`, and the quotient `ι` rule flagged as "semi-builtin" since Lean's kernel cannot add arbitrary computation rules), propositional extensionality, and global choice (with excluded middle derived, not axiomatized) -> `axioms.tex` §"Non-primitive axioms".
- **Undecidability of definitional equality** via a Turing-machine-encoding argument using `acc` (well-foundedness) proofs in an inconsistent local context, and a proof that **algorithmic equality `⇔` is not transitive** (with a second, quotient-based counterexample) and that Lean's algorithmic typing judgment `Vdash` **fails subject reduction** -> `typesys.tex` §"Undecidability of definitional equality" (`sec:undecidable`), §"Algorithmic equality is not transitive", §"Failure of subject reduction".
- Regularity, weakening, and substitution lemmas for the core system -> `typesys.tex` Lemma "Regularity" (`thm:reg`), Lemma "Weakening" (`thm:weak`), Lemma "Properties of substitution" (`thm:subst`), Lemma "Regularity continued" (incl. `item:red_equiv`, `item:alg_defn`).
- **Unique typing** (Theorem `thm:unique` and its indexed refinement `thm:utype`), proved by a joint induction with a **restricted Church-Rosser theorem** (Theorem `thm:church_rosser`, `sec:church_rosser`) for a `β/δ/ζ/ι`-only "kappa" reduction `\rightsquigarrow_\kappa`, factoring definitional equality as `κ`-reduction plus a separate `≡_p` proof-irrelevance/`η` congruence; completeness of this factoring is Theorem `thm:ckappa` -> `unique.tex` throughout (`thm:unique`, `thm:utype`, `thm:0dinv`, `thm:gg_prop`, `thm:gg_compat`, `thm:tri`, `thm:church_rosser`, `thm:ckappa`, `thm:1dinv`).
- Reduction of all Lean inductive types to eight primitives (`⊥, Σ, +, ulift, ‖·‖, W, =, acc`) via an explicit (if partial — introduction rules/recursor/ι-rule-holds-definitionally left as "future work") translation of type families to `W`-types (non-SS case) or to a generalized `acc^φ_r` (SS case) -> `Wtypes.tex` §"The menagerie", §"Translating type families", §"Translating subsingleton eliminators", §"The remainder" (explicit admission of the gap).
- A "proof-split" language separating `∀`/`λ` (propositional, erasable) from `Π`/`Λ` (data-carrying) via `sort`/`lvl` functions built on unique typing, with translation theorems both ways -> `soundness.tex` §"Proof splitting" (start of file through the bijection lemma).
- **Soundness of Lean in ZFC + countably many inaccessibles**: a full interpretation `⟦Γ⊢e⟧_γ` of the proof-split language into sets built from a cumulative hierarchy `U_n` seeded at inaccessible cardinals `κ_n`, covering every type/term former including `W`-types-in-ZFC and `acc`-in-ZFC as explicit transfinite-recursion constructions -> `soundness.tex` §"Modeling Lean in ZFC", Theorem "Soundness" (`thm:sound`), Corollary (Lean's consistency).
- **Type injectivity** refinement using "tagged types" `T_n`/`⟦·⟧` to recover a ZFC analogue of unique typing that the naive set interpretation collapses -> `soundness.tex` §"Type injectivity", Theorem "Soundness" (`thm:sound2`).
- A partial extension of soundness to equality reflection (extensional type theory), abandoned mid-proof (`UNFINISHED`) -> `soundness.tex` §"Equality reflection".
- A sketch (unfinished) of a terminating `K`-only variant `⇝_σ` of the kappa reduction, intended for an actual normalization algorithm -> `normalization.tex` (entire chapter, ends `UNFINISHED`).
- A two-rule-block sketch of **compiling** the type theory to a target with types `\ast` (erased/prop-like) and `\obj` (retained), the closest thing in this document to erasure into an untyped/minimally-typed target -> `compilation.tex` (entire chapter, 25 lines, no supporting prose).

## Detailed walkthrough by chapter

### `axioms.tex` — the axiomatic system

Grammar: levels `ℓ ::= u ∣ 0 ∣ Sℓ ∣ max(ℓ,ℓ) ∣ imax(ℓ,ℓ)`; expressions `e ::= x ∣ U_ℓ ∣ e e ∣ λx:e.e ∣ ∀x:e.e`; contexts `Γ ::= · ∣ Γ,x:e`. The typing judgment `Γ⊢e:α` has the standard weakening/variable/universe-cumulativity/application/lambda/Pi-formation/conversion rules, with `∀`-formation landing in `U_{imax(ℓ1,ℓ2)}` (the `imax` is what gives Lean's `Prop`/impredicativity-adjacent behavior: `imax(ℓ,0)=0` collapses a `∀x:α. Prop`-valued family back into `Prop`). Two auxiliary judgments `Γ⊢α type` and `⊢Γ ok` are defined for convenience and used pervasively in later chapters.

Definitional equality `Γ⊢e≡e'` is built as: an equivalence closure, congruence rules for every constructor, a `β` rule, an `η` rule stated as `λx:α.(e x) ≡ e` when `e:∀x:α.β` (note: NOT the "trivial" instance `λx.(f x)≡f` without a type annotation — the rule requires knowing `e`'s type, which is why unique typing matters for η later), and **proof irrelevance**: `Γ⊢p:P, Γ⊢h:p, Γ⊢h':p ⟹ Γ⊢h≡h'`. Level equality `ℓ≡ℓ'` is defined via a directed inequality judgment `ℓ≤ℓ'+n` (`n∈Z`) with nine syntax-directed rules (monotonicity in `S`, `max` introduction/elimination, three `imax`-normalization identities, and a case-split-on-a-variable rule `ℓ[0/u]≤ℓ'[0/u]+n ∧ ℓ[Su/u]≤ℓ'[Su/u]+n ⟹ ℓ≤ℓ'+n` that makes the decision procedure a structural induction on which universe variable to split on). The semantic reading is spelled out explicitly: levels denote naturals, `⟦imax(m,n)⟧` is `0` if `n=0` else `max(m,n)` — this exact `imax` semantics reappears unchanged in `soundness.tex`.

Algorithmic equality `Γ⊢e⇔e'` drops transitivity and the `β`/`δ`/`ζ`/`ι` rules from the "easy" congruence closure, replacing them with a single head-reduction escape hatch `e⇝k, Γ⊢k⇔e'`, and replaces the `η` rule with an "extensionality principle" `Γ,x:α⊢e x⇔e' x ⟹ Γ⊢e⇔e'` — the text notes this substitution is justified (`⇔` and `≡` agree on this point) but sets up the non-transitivity failure explored in `typesys.tex`.

`ζ`-reduction (`let`) is added as a genuinely non-congruential rule: Lean does not extend the context with definitional equalities for `let`-bound variables, it just zeta-expands whenever needed, and the text gives an explicit conservativity argument (replace every `let` with its expansion and the derivation still goes through in the base system).

`δ`-reduction (definitions) distinguishes `constant c_ū:α` (axiomatic, no reduction rule — "simply an axiomatic extension") from `def c_ū:α:=e` (has a reduction rule, and is a genuinely conservative extension by the same substitute-and-erase argument used for `let`). This constant/axiom vs. definition distinction is exactly the boundary that later chapters (quotients, choice) probe by adding constants that are *not* plain definitions.

Inductive types (`sec:inductive`) get the heaviest treatment: a `spec` judgment for well-formed constructor lists relative to a type family `t:F` in scope, a `ctor` judgment distinguishing non-recursive arguments (type must not mention `t`, but can be used later) from recursive arguments (`∀z::γ. t e`, cannot be referenced in later arguments), and the general recursor's type built from three named components — motive `C:κ` (with `κ` a fresh universe `u` when large-eliminating, else pinned to `P`), minor premises `ε` (one per constructor), and per-recursive-argument premises `δ`. The natural-number example (`N := μN:U_1.(z:N)+(s:N→N)`) is worked fully, including its recursor's signature, and used again in the `sec:iota` section to state the two concrete `ι`-equations before generalizing.

Large elimination (`sec:large_elim`) is characterized by exactly two syntactic sufficient conditions — the family lives at `1≤ℓ`, or it is a **subsingleton (SS)** inductive (single constructor, all non-recursive args either propositional or reappearing in the output index) — with an explicit `LE`/`LEctor` judgment. Both `eq` (`refl`-only single-constructor family) and `acc` (well-foundedness witness) are worked as examples of SS-large-eliminating types with zero and one nonrecursive-but-index-reappearing argument respectively.

The `ι` rule (`sec:iota`) states the standard "recursor on a constructor reduces to the matching minor premise applied to recursive-call results" both as a `≡` fact and as a `⇝` reduction rule, then introduces **K-like reduction** for SS inductives: this is a variant that changes the major premise `h:P a` into a canonically-reconstructed constructor application via proof irrelevance, then applies the ordinary `ι` step — and the text explicitly connects this to "axiom K" via the equality-type instance `rec_{a=} C x a h ≡ x`.

Non-primitive axioms: quotients (`α/R`, `mk_R`, `sound_R`, `lift_R`, with the `ι`-style computation rule `lift_R β f h (mk_R a) ⇝ f a` flagged as *not derivable from a plain definition* — hence "semi-builtin": only `sound_R` is philosophically an "axiom" since the other three constants and the reduction rule would already be satisfied by the degenerate definitions `α/R:=α`, `mk_R a:=a`, `lift_R f h:=f`); propositional extensionality (`propext : ∀p q:P. (p↔q)→p=q`, motivated by CIC's silence on type-level equality of two syntactically-distinct-but-isomorphic inductive definitions of "the same" type); and global choice (`choice:∀α:U_u. nonempty α→α`, with excluded middle noted as *derivable*, not a separate axiom).

### `typesys.tex` — where the naive theory breaks

This short chapter is entirely about documented failures of "nice" properties one might otherwise assume, and is the chapter most directly useful for anyone reasoning about how much trust the *type checker's* accept/reject decision actually buys:

1. **Undecidability of `≡`** (`sec:undecidable`): builds `inv_x : acc x → ∀y. y<x → acc y` by recursion on `acc`, observes `a ≡ intro_acc x (inv_x a)` by proof irrelevance for *any* `a:acc x` in *any* context (including inconsistent ones where `acc_>` is inhabited despite `>` not being well-founded), then encodes an arbitrary `Π^0_1` predicate (a non-halting Turing machine query) as a term `f` such that `f 0 a ≡ ()` iff `∀n. P n`. This shows `≡` (as opposed to `⇝`, which is fine) is undecidable outright — not merely "hard", but genuinely undecidable as a consequence of proof irrelevance interacting with recursion in inconsistent local hypotheses.
2. **`⇔` is not transitive**: the exact same undecidability example, checked step by step, produces `f 0 a ⇔ f 0 (intro_acc 0 (inv_0 a)) ⇔ f 1 (...)` but `f 0 a ⇎ f 1 (...)` directly — because the middle term is a "creative" witness that only exists as an intermediate typechecking step, and `f 0 a` itself is head-normal so the algorithm never explores it. A second, independent source of non-transitivity is exhibited using **quotients of propositions** (a degenerate but legal construction, since quotienting a subsingleton by any relation does nothing semantically, yet `lift` still acts as an SS eliminator syntactically).
3. **Subject reduction fails for the algorithmic judgment `⊩`**: an explicit three-line counterexample chains two individually-checkable `id` applications (each using only one non-transitive `⇔` step) to reach a typing derivation for `id_α (id_β e)` that cannot be replicated for the reduct `id_β e ⇝ e` under `⊩` directly, because `Γ⊩ id_α e : α` would require the single global `α⇔γ` check, which is false, even though the two-hop chain is individually valid. The chapter is explicit that subject reduction *does* hold for the *ideal* judgment `⊢`/`≡` (because `≡` is transitive by construction) — the failure is specific to the decidable approximation.

The chapter closes with the regularity/weakening/substitution lemma package (`thm:reg`, `thm:weak`, `thm:subst`) needed as scaffolding for everything downstream, and a "Regularity continued" package establishing that `⇝` respects `≡`-typing (subject reduction for the ideal reduction relation) and that agreeing-type algorithmic equality implies definitional equality (`item:alg_defn` — "the main reason we are interested in algorithmic equality... it is this that will allow us to conclude that Lean is consistent").

### `unique.tex` — unique typing via an indexed Church-Rosser argument

The chapter opens by naming the circularity problem directly: unique typing's natural proof needs Church-Rosser, but Church-Rosser's natural proof (in a system with proof irrelevance) needs unique typing to identify which terms are "proofs" that get special-cased. The resolution is a joint induction indexed by `n∈N` counting alternations between typing derivations and the conversion rule they invoke (`⊢_n e:α`, `⊢_n α≡β`, defined by mutual recursion on `n`), so that `thm:unique` is really "for every `n`, `⊢_n` has *definitional inversion* [the ability to invert `≡` at `U`/`∀` head symbols] and satisfies unique typing" — proved by induction on `n`, with the `n+1` case for definitional inversion (`thm:1dinv`) built entirely from machinery (Church-Rosser at level `n`, i.e. `thm:ckappa` at level `n`) that is only available once unique typing already holds *at level `n`* (not `n+1`), which is what breaks the circularity.

The **kappa reduction** `⇝_κ` restricts `⇝` to `β/δ/ζ/ι` (dropping the type-dependent `η` and proof-irrelevance rules) and is defined only on terms in **`rec`-normal form** — a canonicalization ensuring every `rec_P`/`lift` subterm carries its full argument count, introduced specifically to prevent a fight between `η`-reduction and `ι`-reduction on partially-applied subsingleton eliminators (the chapter gives the explicit two-way-reducible counterexample `λh:a=a. rec_{a=} C e a h`). The **`K^+` rule** (a "souped-up" K-like reduction usable in the joint induction, contrasted with the simpler `K` rule reintroduced later in `normalization.tex`) needs a context because its side condition `Γ⊢intro inv[p,h]:α` is itself a typing/conversion fact.

Confluence is proved by the Tait–Martin-Löf method: parallel reduction `≫_κ` and "complete" parallel reduction `≫≫_κ` are defined, a **Compatibility lemma** (`thm:gg_compat`) shows `≫_κ` and `≡_p` (the separated proof-irrelevance/η/universe-changing congruence, defined with an explicit non-symmetric two-sided `η` rule because `≡_p` itself lacks a symmetry rule) commute up to a square, then the **Triangle lemma** (`thm:tri`) is proved by simultaneous induction/inversion over every substantive `≫_κ`/`≫≫_κ` rule (β, δ, ζ, non-SS `ι`, quotient `ι`, `K^+`, and every compatibility rule) — the proof is long precisely because each of the 7+ rule shapes needs its own case. Church-Rosser (`thm:church_rosser`) for `⇝_κ` follows in the usual four-step lift (single-step-diamond → many-step-vs-one-step → many-step-vs-many-step → convert `≫` back to `⇝`).

`thm:ckappa` ("Completeness of the κ reduction": `Γ⊢e≡e' ⟺ Γ⊢e≡_κ e'`, where `≡_κ` is "reduce both sides via `⇝_κ` to `⇝_κ`-normal forms that are `≡_p`-equal") is the theorem that actually gets used for `thm:1dinv`'s case analysis: because `U_ℓ` and `∀x:α.β` have no `⇝_κ`-redexes at the head, any `≡_κ` relating two such terms must bottom out either in the compatibility rule (giving the desired sub-relations directly) or in `≡_p` via proof irrelevance — the latter case is always refuted by deriving `0≡S²ℓ` or similar via a second application of unique-typing-at-level-`n` to the terms' own universe types (e.g. `U_ℓ:U_{Sℓ}:U_{SSℓ}` forces `P≡U_{SSℓ}` in the proof-irrelevance branch, contradicting definitional inversion for universes). This "derive a universe-level absurdity from the wrong branch" pattern repeats identically in all three cases of `thm:1dinv`.

### `Wtypes.tex` — reducing everything to eight primitives

Motivated purely as a proof-simplification device for the ZFC soundness argument (rather than as anything Lean actually does internally), this chapter shows the entire inductive-type apparatus of `axioms.tex` §"Inductive types" is inter-derivable with eight fixed primitive formers: `⊥`, `Σ`, `+`, `ulift` (universe lifting `U_u→U_v` for `u≤v`), `‖·‖` (propositional truncation / `nonempty`), `W` (the classical W-type `Wx:A.B := μw:U_ℓ.(sup:∀x:A.(B→w)→w)`), `=` (equality as an SS-inductive family), and `acc`. Each is given both a direct rule-set and an explicit unfolding as a `μt:F.K` term, and each comes with `η`-style rules (`↑↓x≡x`, `(π1 x,π2 x)≡x`) that the text is explicit are **not** actual Lean definitional equalities but are needed for the reduction and are adopted "for soundness only" as a "modest strengthening" — an explicitly flagged gap between the axiomatized system used for soundness and Lean's real system.

The translation itself proceeds in two cases. For families landing at `1≤ℓ` (no small elimination concerns), the "skeleton + predicate" method builds a plain `W`-type skeleton `S` erasing all indices, then a `good:S→∀x::α.P` predicate carving out index-correct members recursively over `S`'s shape, worked through concretely on a two-constructor example (`X = μt:N→U_1.(one:t 1)+(double:∀n. t n→t(2n))`) before the general `spec⇒A;B;G` translation judgment is given (four rules: empty spec, cons, non-recursive-arg extension via `Σ`, recursive-arg extension via `Σ(z::γ)+B` conjoined with an equality obligation). For the **subsingleton** case, `W`-types cannot be used at all (propositional truncation would destroy large elimination, which SS types still need), so the translation instead packs the whole index tuple into one `acc^φ_r` instance, with `φ` collecting the propositional/index-equality constraints and `r` the recursive-call edge relation, again given as a syntax-directed `LEctor ⇒ x.B; x'x.[S;R]` judgment. A fully worked binary-family example (`P = μt:N→N→P.(intro:∀n. n>2 → (∀m. m<n→t n m) → t 0 n)`) walks through the packing.

The chapter is explicit about what it leaves undone: "it still remains to define the introduction rules and the recursor, and show that the iota rule holds definitionally with these definitions... this will be left as future work, probably as part of a formalization of all of this" (§"The remainder") — i.e. only the *type-former* translation is completed; the term-level (constructor/eliminator) translation for the general case is an acknowledged, explicit gap in this document.

### `soundness.tex` — the longest chapter, in three movements

**Movement 1 (proof splitting)**: because the interpretation needs to know statically which subterms are "real" (data-carrying) versus proof-irrelevant placeholders, the language is duplicated into `∀`/`λ`/application (propositional, always interpreted as the single proof-token `•`) versus `Π`/`Λ`/`·`-application (data, interpreted faithfully), routed by two functions `sort`(term)/`lvl`(type) whose *well-definedness is a direct corollary of unique typing* — spelled out as its own three-part lemma ("The lvl and sort functions") before the translation `⟨e⟩_Γ` is defined by cases on `sort`/`lvl` at each binder/application. Both directions of translation (forward `⟨·⟩`, reverse `¯·`) are shown type- and equality-preserving, and a bijection-up-to-level-substitution lemma is proved, establishing that the proof-split language is a faithful, unique-typing-possessing stand-in for the original for any *fixed* universe valuation (the text notes explicitly this loses universe-polymorphism generality but that's fine "when the universes have been fixed... in evaluation and in proofs of contradiction").

**Movement 2 (the ZFC model)**: fixes an increasing sequence of uncountable strong-limit cardinals `(κ_n)`, calls it "n-correct" if `κ_0,...,κ_{n-1}` are all inaccessible, builds a cumulative universe hierarchy `U_0={∅,{•}}`, `U_{n+1}=V_{κ_n}`, `U_ω=⋃U_n`, fixes a global choice function `ε` on `U_ω`. The interpretation `⟦Γ⊢e⟧_γ` is defined by a well-founded (measure-based, explicitly given size function `|e|`, `|Γ|`, `|Γ⊢e|`, with special-cased shrinking measures for `let` and `δ`-unfolding so the recursion terminates through those non-structural steps) mutual recursion covering every single term-former from both `axioms.tex` and `Wtypes.tex` — propositions interpret into `{∅,{•}}`-valued subsets, `Π` into genuine dependent products, `Σ`/`+`/`ulift`/`‖·‖`/`W`/`=`/`acc`/quotients each get a dedicated clause, with `W`-types-in-ZFC and `acc`-in-ZFC each spelled out as their own subsections (`W` via a smallest-fixed-point/rank argument plus a transfinite-recursion-defined recursor; `acc` via the standard well-founded-restriction-of-a-relation construction, with the recursor built by recursion along the `acc`-restricted relation). Soundness (`thm:sound`) is then a single large case-by-case induction (one bullet per syntax former, ~35 bullets) proving simultaneously that props interpret into subsets of `{•}`, level-0 (propositional) terms interpret to `•`, well-typed terms land in their type's interpretation *given sufficient cardinal correctness* `k` (existentially quantified, "essentially just the max of all universe numbers that appear in the course of the proof"), and `≡`-equal terms interpret identically. The **consistency corollary** is immediate: any closed proof of `⊥` interprets into `∅`, contradiction.

**Movement 3 (type injectivity, and the abandoned reflection extension)**: notes that the naive ZFC interpretation collapses distinct-but-definitionally-inequal types into the same underlying set (ZFC doesn't remember "how" a type was built), which would break a would-be ZFC-level unique-typing property; fixes this with a parallel "tagged type" hierarchy `T_n⊆U_n` with an untagging function `⟦t⟧`, generated by five clauses (universe-as-a-tag, `Π`, `Σ`, `W`, `+`, `ulift`) that is shown to embed faithfully in `U_n`, then restates soundness (`thm:sound2`) using tags, with a one-line proof ("virtually unchanged... none of the tags affect any of the reasoning"). The final section attempts to extend soundness to **equality reflection** (`Γ⊢h:e=e' ⟹ Γ⊢e≡e'`, the defining feature of *extensional* type theories, which Lean deliberately lacks) by treating reflection as another index-raising rule in the `⊢_n` hierarchy from `unique.tex`, requiring soundness and unique typing to be proved in one combined induction this time (rather than sequentially, since reflection breaks the standalone unique-typing argument by injecting arbitrary equality-derived conversions) — the proof of the combined four-part theorem is started (base case `n=0` is dispatched for parts 1 and 3) and then **stops mid-sentence** at the inductive step for part 2, terminating in the literal token `UNFINISHED`. This is the single largest explicitly-flagged gap in the whole document.

### `normalization.tex` and `compilation.tex` — the two abandoned chapters

`normalization.tex` diagnoses why `⇝_κ`'s `K^+` rule is useless as a normalization/decision procedure despite being fine for the Church-Rosser proof: `K^+` unconditionally rewrites *any* `rec_P` application (regardless of whether its major premise is a constructor) into a longer term that itself typically contains another `rec_P` redex, so applying it eagerly does not terminate — the text notes this was "sufficiently powerful to act as a semi-decision procedure for definitional equality where existing methods fail" precisely because it doesn't terminate. The proposed fix, `⇝_σ`, reinstates the *original* narrow `K` rule (applies only when no fresh `inv_i h` projection terms are produced on the right, i.e. only when the major premise's SS-ness is witnessed purely by data already present in the non-recursive parameters `p`) plus the ordinary `ι` rule for all inductives (dropping the non-SS/SS split that `⇝_κ` needed) and the ordinary congruence/β/δ/ζ rules. The chapter ends immediately after stating the `K` rule, with no termination argument, no relation to `⇝_κ`/`≡` established, and the literal token `UNFINISHED`.

`compilation.tex` is the shortest and thinnest chapter: it gives a target-type grammar `τ::=∗∣obj∣τ→τ` and two small rule blocks. The **type translation** `Γ⊢α⇒τ` sends a `∀`-type to `∗` if its codomain translates to `∗` (i.e. it is itself erasable, so the whole thing is treated as a proof-shaped object) and otherwise to an arrow `α'→β'`; sends every `U_ℓ` to `∗`; allows translating via an intermediate `⇝`-reduct; and gives two special-cased rules for saturated inductive-family applications — a family landing in `P` (i.e. `μt:(∀x::α.P).K` applied to indices) translates to `∗`, while a family landing at a nonzero-level universe translates to `obj`. The **expression translation** (labeled just `e::=x∣` with no further grammar given, itself a sign of incompleteness) restates the variable/`U_ℓ`/`∀`-type rules as term-level rules (all landing back in `τ`, i.e. this fragment does not yet distinguish "the type of a term" from "the erasure of a term" cleanly) and repeats the one inductive-family-application rule. No rule for `λ`, application, `rec_P`, `let`, or any of the `Wtypes.tex`/quotient/choice formers is given; there is no environment-erasure construction, no target-language operational semantics, and no stated correctness property (forward simulation or otherwise) connecting source reduction/typing to the `∗`/`obj` target. This chapter should be read strictly as an unfinished proof-of-concept sketch, not as a specification of any actual erasure procedure.

## Every place the thesis notes an unproved or informal step

The task instructions ask specifically for this, so it is broken out as its own list (each item cross-references the fuller discussion above):

1. **`Wtypes.tex` §"The remainder" (end of chapter, explicit prose admission)**: "it still remains to define the introduction rules and the recursor, and show that the iota rule holds definitionally with these definitions... this will be left as future work, probably as part of a formalization of all of this." Only the *type-former* half of the general-inductive-to-eight-primitives reduction is completed; the term-level (constructors + recursor + `ι`-rule-holds-definitionally) half is an acknowledged, open gap.
2. **`Wtypes.tex` §"The menagerie" (mid-chapter, explicit prose admission of a deliberate strengthening)**: the two `η`-style rules `↑↓x≡x` and `(π1 x,π2 x)≡x` are stated to be "provable but not definitional equalities in Lean"; the text says "since we are going for soundness only, we will help ourselves to this modest strengthening of the system" — i.e. the soundness proof is carried out for a system that is *not quite* Lean's real definitional equality, by the author's own explicit statement, and the reason given is that without it "we would only be able to go as far as indexed W-types, which are more complex."
3. **`soundness.tex` §"Equality reflection" (end of chapter)**: the extension of soundness to extensional Lean-with-reflection is attempted but the proof is abandoned mid-sentence — "Now suppose the induction hypothesis holds for $n$. From (1), if $\Gamma\vdash e:$" — followed by the literal token `UNFINISHED`. Only the base case (`n=0`, two of the four theorem parts) is actually discharged.
4. **`normalization.tex` (entire chapter, ends abruptly)**: the chapter's stated goal is to give a terminating replacement for the (Church-Rosser-compatible but non-terminating) `K^+` rule, suitable for an actual normalization/decision procedure. It defines the candidate relation `⇝_σ` (restricted `K`, standard `ι`) and stops immediately afterward — no termination theorem, no confluence-of-`⇝_σ` theorem, and no theorem relating `⇝_σ`-normal forms back to `≡`/`⇔` is stated or attempted. Ends with the literal token `UNFINISHED`.
5. **`compilation.tex` (entire chapter, 25 lines)**: presented with zero connecting prose. The type-translation rules cover `∀`, `U_ℓ`, an `⇝`-then-translate rule, and saturated inductive-family application only; the expression-translation rules cover only variables, `U_ℓ`, `∀` (as a term, oddly — likely a `typo`/incompleteness in the source itself, since `∀x:α.β` is not usually a term needing a *term*-level translation distinct from its type-level translation) and the same one inductive-family rule repeated. No rule appears for `λ`, application, `let`, `rec_P` (of any kind), or any `Wtypes.tex` primitive, quotient, or choice term. There is no environment-erasure definition and no stated erasure-correctness theorem (no forward simulation, no target semantics at all — `τ::=∗∣obj∣τ→τ` is given no operational meaning). This is the weakest-substantiated chapter in the source and should not be cited as if it specifies a complete or verified erasure procedure.
6. **`soundness.tex` §"Soundness" proof, opening remark (informal aside, not a gap but a documented non-rigor)**: "The proof is constructive for the value of $k$... We will not spend much time discussing it... (There is nothing surprising in this proof, except perhaps the fact that I took the trouble to write it down.)" — the author explicitly flags the ~35-case main soundness induction as routine/tedious rather than conceptually hard, and does not track the exact existential bound `k` case-by-case (leaves it as "the max of all universe numbers that appear").
7. **`typesys.tex` §"Regularity" and other "easy" lemmas (recurring informal-proof pattern)**: several lemmas in `typesys.tex` and `unique.tex` are proved with one-line justifications such as "By induction on the respective judgments (all of the parts may be proven separately)" or "All parts are easy inductions" — these are not gaps in the mathematical content (the results are standard and the proof sketches are almost certainly correct and mechanizable) but they are *informal* in the sense of not being spelled out, which matters for anyone trying to reconstruct a fully formal (e.g. Lean/Rocq-checked) version of this metatheory, as lean4lean's own verification effort must.

Taken together, points 1–5 delineate a specific, load-bearing boundary: **this document's *proved* content stops at unique typing + Church-Rosser + the eight-primitives type-former reduction + full ZFC soundness of the ideal (non-reflective) calculus.** Everything past that — the general inductive-type recursor/ι-rule reduction, soundness with reflection, a terminating normalization procedure, and any erasure/compilation correctness theorem — is either partially sketched or entirely absent in this source. For lean-to-lambdabox purposes specifically: this thesis supplies zero erasure-correctness content; `compilation.tex` cannot be cited as evidence for, or a specification of, any part of the shipping Lean eraser or its verification.

## Selected rule statements (verbatim-in-spirit, for direct citation)

These are transcribed from the LaTeX source (unicode-ized) for cases where later work will want to quote the exact formal statement rather than a paraphrase.

- **Conversion rule** (`axioms.tex`, boxed judgment `Γ⊢e:α`): `Γ⊢e:α, Γ⊢α≡β ⟹ Γ⊢e:β`.
- **Pi/forall formation** (`axioms.tex`): `Γ⊢α:U_{ℓ1}, Γ,x:α⊢β:U_{ℓ2} ⟹ Γ⊢∀x:α.β : U_{imax(ℓ1,ℓ2)}`.
- **Proof irrelevance** (`axioms.tex`, definitional equality block): `Γ⊢p:P, Γ⊢h:p, Γ⊢h':p ⟹ Γ⊢h≡h'`.
- **η rule** (`axioms.tex`): `Γ⊢e:∀y:α.β ⟹ Γ⊢λx:α.(e x) ≡ e`.
- **Algorithmic extensionality (replaces η in `⇔`)** (`axioms.tex`): `Γ,x:α⊢e x ↔ e' x ⟹ Γ⊢e ⇔ e'` (note: the source writes the hypothesis with `↔`/head-reduction-equivalence notation `e x ↔ e' x`, distinct from `≡`).
- **Head-reduction escape hatch for `⇔`** (`axioms.tex`): `e⇝k, Γ⊢k⇔e' ⟹ Γ⊢e⇔e'` — the *only* place `⇔` invokes `β`/`δ`/`ι`/`ζ` reduction.
- **δ-reduction admissibility** (`axioms.tex`): `def c_ū:α:=e` admissible iff `⊢e:α` and all universe variables in `e,α` are contained in `ū`.
- **General recursor typing** (`axioms.tex` §"The recursor"): `Γ,t:F⊢K spec ⟹ Γ⊢rec_P : ∀C:κ. ∀e::ε. ∀a::α. ∀z:P a. C a z`, with `κ = ∀a::α. P a → U_u` (fresh `u`) if `K` is large-eliminating, else `κ = ∀a::α. P a → P`.
- **Quotient computation rule** (`axioms.tex`): `lift_R β f h (mk_R a) ⇝ f a` — explicitly called out as a rule, not a constant, hence the "semi-builtin" classification of the quotient axioms as a group.
- **Unique typing** (`unique.tex`, `thm:unique`): `Γ⊢e:α, Γ⊢e:β ⟹ Γ⊢α≡β`.
- **Church-Rosser for κ-reduction** (`unique.tex`, `thm:church_rosser`): `Γ⊢e:α, e⇝*_κ e1, e⇝*_κ e2 ⟹ ∃e1',e2'. Γ⊢e1'≡_p e2' ∧ e1⇝*_κ e1' ∧ e2⇝*_κ e2'`.
- **Completeness of κ-reduction** (`unique.tex`, `thm:ckappa`): `Γ⊢e≡e' ⟺ Γ⊢e≡_κ e'`, where `≡_κ` is "both sides `⇝*_κ`-reduce to `≡_p`-equal terms".
- **Soundness (tagged version, the "final" form)** (`soundness.tex`, `thm:sound2`): `Γ⊢e:α ⟹ ∃k. (κ-sequence is k-correct) ⟹ ∀γ∈⟦Γ⟧. ⟦Γ⊢e⟧_γ ∈ ⟦⟦Γ⊢α⟧_γ⟧` (double-bracket = untagging the tagged-type interpretation).
- **Consistency corollary** (`soundness.tex`, unlabeled corollary after `thm:sound`): "Lean is consistent if ZFC + {there are $n$ inaccessible cardinals ∣ n∈ω} is. That is, there is no proof of `⊥` that is verified by the Lean kernel."
- **Compilation type-erasure rule for large-eliminating families** (`compilation.tex`, the one rule most relevant to a λ□ comparison): `ℓ≢0 ⟹ Γ⊢(μt:(∀x::α.U_ℓ).K) e ⇒ obj` (data-carrying), contrasted with `Γ⊢(μt:(∀x::α.P).K) e ⇒ ∗` (erased) for prop-valued families — this is the thesis's only concrete, rule-form analogue of a `tBox`-vs-kept-term erasability decision, and it is decided purely from the family's *target universe*, not from an `isErasable`-style traversal of the specific term as MetaRocq's erasure does.

## Why this document matters for the lean-to-lambdabox verification effort

None of this thesis's *content* is reusable directly as a citation for erasure correctness — it establishes none of that. Its relevance is as **grounding for assumptions the Lean eraser's correctness proof is entitled to make about the source calculus**, in three specific ways:

1. **Unique typing is a real theorem about Lean's ideal typing judgment, proved here from scratch, but it is proved for `⊢`/`≡`, not for the algorithmic `⊩`/`⇔` that the actual kernel (and hence lean4lean) decides.** Anyone building on "Lean's typechecker accepted this, therefore the term has a well-defined type/erasure-classification" is implicitly relying on `item:alg_defn` (`typesys.tex`) — the one-directional fact that agreeing-type algorithmic equality implies definitional equality — not on completeness of the algorithm. This thesis is the place that fact is stated and proved, and also the place its limits (undecidability of `≡`, non-transitivity of `⇔`, failure of subject reduction for `⊩`) are documented; a Lean-to-λ□ correctness argument that implicitly assumes "the kernel is a decision procedure for `≡`" would be assuming something this thesis explicitly disproves.
2. **The `sort`/`lvl` erasability-adjacent machinery in `soundness.tex` depends on unique typing being available, and is scoped to a *fixed* universe valuation.** Any Lean erasure procedure that needs to decide "is this subterm data or proof/erasable" at a universe-polymorphic declaration (i.e. before choosing a valuation) is working in a regime this thesis's own `sort`/`lvl` machinery does not directly cover (per the explicit remark closing §"Proof splitting"); this is a genuine, citable scope boundary rather than an oversight, and it lines up with the polymorphic-level concerns raised elsewhere in this project's own worklog (see this session's MEMORY.md note "Round7 2026-09" about `ErasesEnv.defns` at polymorphic bodies on the MetaRocq/erasure side — the analogous concern surfaces on the Lean-kernel-theory side here).
3. **`compilation.tex`'s single erasure-relevant rule (large-eliminating-family ⇒ `obj` vs. prop-family ⇒ `∗`, decided by the family's target universe) is a strictly weaker and less complete decision procedure than an `isErasable`-style per-term traversal.** It should not be cited as evidence that "universe-based erasure suffices for Lean," since the chapter never proves any correctness property for it, never handles `λ`/application/`rec_P`/`let`, and is only 25 lines with no term-level erasure function at all.
