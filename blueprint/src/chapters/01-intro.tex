\section*{At a glance}
\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Goal} Verify, in Lean 4, the shipping \code{\#erase} eraser --- \code{Erasure.erase}
(\code{Erasure.lean:927}) and its term walk \code{Erasure.visitExpr} (\code{:590}), the code that
writes the \code{.ast} files the benchmark suite measures --- \hl{not an idealised model of it}.
\item \lead{Architecture} Follows Sozeau et al.'s certified erasure for Rocq ([S, Sec. 7.3--7.4],
after Letouzey [L]): a target calculus with weak call-by-value semantics; an erasability
specification and non-deterministic erasure relation refined by the implemented function; a forward
simulation; a collapse of non-determinism at first-order observations --- re-formalised for Lean's
term language and, via \code{lean4lean}, its kernel theory.
\item \lead{Main results} Theorem~\ref{thm:shipping_erase_correct_firstorder}
(\code{shipping\_erase\_correct\_firstorder}, Chapter~\ref{chap:capstone}): first-order source evaluation
is matched by the emitted program's evaluation.
\item \lead{Status} \stProved{} in Lean, no local \code{sorry}, but \textbf{conditional}: ten of fifteen
binders are hypotheses; \stOpen{} on \code{hbridge}; \stInherited{} throughout, via \code{lean4lean}.
\item \lead{Lean files} \code{Erasure.lean}, \code{Capstone.lean}, \code{ErasureSpec.lean},
\code{Green.lean}.
\item \lead{Paper} [S, Sec. 7.3--7.4]; [L]; the eraser's own design document is [R]
(Section~\ref{sec:intro:references}).
\end{itemize}

\section{The problem and the object of verification}
\label{sec:intro:problem}

\lead{In short} Peregrine's Lean frontend erases \code{Lean.Expr} to \lbox{} through one entry point,
\code{Erasure.erase}; this blueprint verifies that shipping function, not a model of it.

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Pipeline} Peregrine compiles proof-assistant programs through one Rocq-verified
middle-end whose input is \lbox{}; a frontend's only job is to produce that program. This repository
is the Lean frontend.
\item \lead{Entry point} \code{\#erase} (\code{Erasure.lean:982}, elaborated by
\code{Erasure.eraseElab}, \code{:985}) takes a term plus an optional configuration, output file and
\code{.mli} sidecar; it elaborates, calls \code{Erasure.erase} and serialises the result.
\item \code{Erasure.erase} runs \code{Erasure.visitExpr} on the output of
\code{Erasure.prepare\_erasure}, from the empty state, returning the untyped program (declarations
plus main term) and inlining hints. The main theorem's two run hypotheses are exactly this split;
\code{erase\_run\_ok} (Chapter~\ref{chap:coldstart}) performs it formally.
\item \lead{Preparation} \code{Erasure.prepare\_erasure} makes up to four rewrites: redirect
constants to their \code{\_unsafe\_rec} companions (\code{replaceUnsafeRecNames}); Lean's LCNF
passes \code{macroInline}, \code{inlineMatchers}; and, only when \code{csimp} is on, \code{@[csimp]}
replacements. The syntactic conjuncts speak of the \emph{prepared} term, the observable conjunct of
the term the user wrote; \code{prepare\_sound} connects the two. The eraser also reads a
compiler-facing body (the \code{\_unsafe\_rec} companion, top-level recursive), not the
kernel-facing recursor-based one; both are named hypotheses, not silent assumptions.
\end{itemize}

\section{The main theorem in one paragraph}
\label{sec:intro:theorem}

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Given} the shipping eraser runs on $e$ from the empty erasure state under the pinned
configuration and returns $(\Gamma, t)$; the prepared term $pe$ it actually walks lies in the
supported fragment and translates into \code{lean4lean}'s model of the environment; the emitted
program declares a body for every constant it reaches and satisfies peregrine's first-pass check;
\item \lead{Then} there are $\Gamma_{\mathrm{spec}}$ and an intermediate \lbox{} term $t_0$, with $pe$
erasing to $t_0$ (\Erases{}), $\Gamma_{\mathrm{spec}}$ erasing the source environment along everything
$t_0$ reaches (\ErasesEnv{}), and \Lower{} taking $t_0$ to $t$ and $\Gamma_{\mathrm{spec}}$ to
$\Gamma$;
\item \lead{The observable} for every closed argument spine whose \lbox{} images carry erasures of
their own: if $e$ applied to it evaluates to a value $v$ whose type is a spine of an inductive
\emph{first order} in the model, then $v$ has a unique erasure, that erasure's lowering is box-free,
and \hl{the emitted program's evaluation of $t$ at the lowered arguments equals it exactly}, under
\lbox{}'s weak call-by-value semantics.
\end{itemize}

\code{shipping\_erase\_correct\_firstorder} (\code{Capstone.lean:146}) is stated in full as
Theorem~\ref{thm:shipping_erase_correct_firstorder} in Chapter~\ref{chap:capstone}.

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Conditional} Fifteen explicit binders. Five are discharged by checked terms at all
eight instances (pinned configuration, translation, fragment verdict, body-less reference check,
output well-formedness), a sixth (compiler-body) at the first instance; the remaining ten are
hypotheses. One, \code{hbridge} -- the two environment fields of \code{ErasureBridge}
(\code{Capstone.lean:101}) -- is \stOpen{}: discharged nowhere.
\item \lead{Eight instances} \code{Green.green\_G1} through \code{green\_G8}
(\code{LeanToLambdaBox/Green.lean}), each a real \code{\#erase} run with a committed emitted
program whose conclusion ends in a literal Peano numeral (so it cannot be $\Box$ or a stuck term).
Environments are universally quantified: \emph{conditional} non-vacuity only.
\item \lead{Not concluded} MetaRocq's \code{expanded\_eprogram} (here \code{LBExpandedFix}), hence
not \code{PeregrinePre}: every emitted recursive body is a bare fixpoint node, fifty of fifty
measured (finding F-ETA). Concludes \code{LBWfPeregrine} (\code{Output.lean:227}) instead, which
the emitted programs satisfy.
\end{itemize}

\section{The architecture of the proof}
\label{sec:intro:architecture}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \lead{Target and semantics} (Chapter~\ref{chap:lambdabox}). \code{LBTerm}
(\code{Basic.lean:90}) is MetaRocq's \code{EAst.term} plus a free-variable and a primitive-value
constructor, no cofixpoints. \WcbvEval{} (\code{Semantics/Eval.lean:79}) re-formalises
\code{EWcbvEval}, flag-parameterised, stated at the erasure flag point and carried to the entry
flag point peregrine declares for its input. \code{lbEval\_sound}
(\code{Semantics/Compute.lean:497}) turns each instance's evaluation into a kernel computation.
\item \lead{Source side} (Chapter~\ref{chap:source}). Every typing, definitional-equality and
translation fact is \code{lean4lean}'s. \SEval{} (\code{SourceEval.lean:193}) sits on top, its
$\delta$ arm reading the compiler-body table, with subject reduction \code{SEval.defeq}. The
eraser's fragment is \code{Supported} (\code{Supported.lean:591}), checker \code{supportedB\_sound}.
\item \lead{Erasure specification} (Chapter~\ref{chap:erases}). \Erasable{}
(\code{Erasability.lean:55}) is [S]'s \code{isErasable}. \Erases{} (\code{Erases.lean:211}),
[S, Fig. 18]: eleven rules, ten congruences plus one non-deterministic box rule. \ErasesEnv{}
(\code{ErasesEnv.lean:44}), [S]'s \code{erases\_deps}, is one clause with seven conditions.
\item \lead{Forward simulation} (Chapter~\ref{chap:correctness}). \code{erases\_correct}
(\code{Close.lean:29}), [S]'s theorem of the same name: one induction on the source evaluation, the
$\iota$, $\delta$ and projection arms as step lemmas. One simulation, on the composite.
\item \lead{Lowering} (Chapter~\ref{chap:lowering}). \Lower{} (\code{Lower.lean:336}), fourteen
arms, relates two \lbox{} terms. Carries the four Lean-specific steps: constructor constants to
heads, saturated \code{casesOn} spines to case nodes, block members to fixpoint nodes; \LowerEnv{}
prunes the environment. \code{LBWfPeregrine} (twelve clauses) is decided by
\code{lbWfPeregrine\_of\_check}.
\item \lead{Run model and refinement} (Chapters~\ref{chap:eraser}, \ref{chap:run},
\ref{chap:refinement}). \code{ErasureRun.lean} (3620 lines) is the algebra of the eraser's monadic
runs. \code{visitExpr\_refines\_erasesLB} and its fixpoint companion say a successful run lands in
composite \code{ErasesLB} --- the analogue of [S]'s \code{erases\_erase} and [L, Lemma 11]. One
eighteen-motive fixpoint induction whose steps the capstone supplies as proved terms.
\item \lead{Cold start and bridge} (Chapter~\ref{chap:coldstart}). \code{erase\_run\_ok} splits the
entry point into preparation and term walk; \code{prepare\_sound} transports the source evaluation
across it; \code{erasure\_bridge\_of\_run} (\code{Capstone.lean:63}) instantiates the bridge
invariant at the empty entry state and applies the refinement with all eighteen steps supplied,
concluding the erasure half. It cannot supply \code{ErasureBridge}'s two environment fields;
\code{bridgeEnv\_of\_regInv} (\code{Capstone.lean:122}) would derive them from a registration
invariant no theorem yet establishes.
\item \lead{Capstone and instances} (Chapter~\ref{chap:capstone}). The composition, plus the eight
instances: \code{erases\_correct} once at the spine, \code{firstorder\_erases\_core} for the
answer's constructor-tree shape and uniqueness, \code{noBox\_lower\_of\_foSpine} for box-freedom.
\end{enumerate}

Everything above is proved \emph{from} the hypotheses of Chapter~\ref{chap:trust}: the graph in this
blueprint shows the proofs, and the assumption nodes show where the proofs stop.

Five deviations matter most.

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Applied-form constructors} (Chapter~\ref{chap:lambdabox}). peregrine's first pass needs
applied form; the erasure flag point pins the non-block regime and \Erases{}'s constructor rule
emits a bare head. [L] has no such distinction (constructors are context variables there); [S] does
not discuss it. Measured: all 982 emitted constructor nodes carry an empty argument list.
\item \lead{No case or fixpoint rule in \Erases{}} (Chapter~\ref{chap:erases}). Structural: Lean
writes constructors, inductive names and constants all as \code{Expr.const}.
\item \lead{No emitted fixpoint is applied} (Chapter~\ref{chap:lowering}). The eraser emits a
fixpoint node as a whole constant body, so \code{expanded\_eprogram} is false on all five benchmark
programs (F-ETA) --- the gap between concluded \code{LBWfPeregrine} and unconcluded
\code{PeregrinePre}.
\item \lead{The first-order fragment} (Chapters~\ref{chap:correctness}, \ref{chap:trust}). Both
papers restrict the observation, not the program; so does this development. But first-orderness of
the naturals is a hypothesis at all eight instances, and the predicate's only proved model
inhabitant here is an empty inductive.
\item \lead{Machine \code{Nat}, \code{@[csimp]}, \code{@[extern]}} (Chapter~\ref{chap:trust}). All
three are shipping features excluded by \code{ConfigPinned}, and no theorem in this blueprint
covers a run with any of them enabled.
\end{itemize}

\section{How to read this blueprint}
\label{sec:intro:legend}

\begin{description}
\item[\lead{Node kinds}] Five standard environments (\emph{definition}, \emph{lemma},
\emph{proposition}, \emph{theorem}, \emph{corollary}) plus \emph{assumption}, added here: assumed
rather than proved, living in Chapter~\ref{chap:trust} only. Remarks are prose, not graph nodes.
\item[\lead{Graph colours}] \code{leanblueprint}'s own. Border: green (statement formalised), blue
(prerequisites formalised, not the statement), orange (not-ready). Fill: green (proof complete),
blue (ready to attempt), dark green (node and every ancestor proved).
\item[\lead{Status badges}] \stProved{} proved in this repository; \stChecked{} discharged by a
checked term or kernel computation at a rung; \stAssumed{} a hypothesis binder or bundle field;
\stInherited{} trust inherited from \code{lean4lean}'s \code{sorry}s or Lean's reflection axioms;
\stOpen{} an open proof obligation; \stOutside{} outside the verified perimeter.
\item[\lead{What green means here}] No \code{sorry} of its own, so every result node in
Chapters~\ref{chap:lambdabox}--\ref{chap:capstone} is green in both senses. \hl{Green means ``proved
from the hypotheses of its own statement,'' never ``unconditionally true''} --- for the capstone it
does not mean the erasure is correct. The hypotheses are Chapter~\ref{chap:trust}'s assumption
nodes, carrying a Lean name when a Lean declaration states them and otherwise no proof environment
at all, so every path into the capstone visibly passes through one.
\item[\lead{Four hypothesis bundles}]~\\
\begin{tabular}{llp{0.67\linewidth}}
\code{ErasureSpec} & 8 fields & Lean \code{Meta}/\code{Core} primitives, model connection \\
\code{EraserAsks} & 5 fields & this repository's own code; 2 (kernel inductive head, block-key
distinctness) refuted in general (F-DEPTH, F-UNSAFEREC), hold only on measured inputs \\
\code{UpstreamAsks} & 4 fields & \code{lean4lean} lemmas a pin bump would supply \\
\code{ErasureBridge} & 2 fields & the open problem \\
\end{tabular}\\
The theorem's inline binders are named one by one in Chapter~\ref{chap:trust}.
\item[\lead{The \code{lean4lean} trust boundary}] Every typing, definitional-equality and
term-translation fact on the source side is \code{lean4lean}'s. The pin carries sixteen
\code{sorry} sites, enumerated in \code{test/lean4lean-sorries.expected}, re-measured by
\code{scripts/lean4lean-sorries.sh}; results reaching them inherit \code{sorryAx}. The one
\stInherited{} boundary here, as [S] inherits a normalization axiom, and it is an assumption node
with no Lean counterpart in Chapter~\ref{chap:trust}.
\item[\lead{Measurement, not narration}] \code{test/ledger.expected} holds the literal
\code{\#print axioms} output of forty invocations in \code{test/Ledger.lean}, CI diffs it. The
capstone and each instance carry the same 33 names, including \code{sorryAx} and twenty-nine
non-standard reflection axioms of Lean's executable kernel --- the price of discharging the
relevance oracle through \code{lean4lean}'s checker. \code{visitExpr\_refines\_erasesLB},
\code{bridgeEnv\_of\_regInv}, \code{LBOptimize\_correct} and \code{lbEval\_sound} measure free of
\code{sorryAx}. Moving an obligation into a binder moves no row.
\item[\lead{One further rule}] A statement more specific than its prose is flagged after the
informal one; deviations from [S] or [L] are always named.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Deviations from the paper and caveats.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Deviation} No case rule and no fixpoint rule; the steps that build those nodes live in
\Lower{}, with no counterpart in either paper.
\item \lead{Caveat} Correctness is stated off a \emph{hypothesised} source evaluation: nothing here
proves a Lean term evaluates, only that if it does, the emitted program agrees.
\item \lead{Caveat} The target semantics is faithful to MetaRocq only at the non-block flag point;
$\iota$ and projection drop MetaRocq's parameter-count and saturation side conditions.
\item \lead{Caveat} \code{hnb} is bound and never used in its proof; \code{hblk}, though forwarded
to \code{erasure\_bridge\_of\_run}, is spent by no theorem in the closure --- two premises stronger
than the proof requires.
\end{itemize}

Chapter~\ref{chap:trust} records these, and every other finding, with its measurement.
