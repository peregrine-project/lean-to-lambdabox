\section{What is verified}\label{sec:intro-what}

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{The eraser} \code{lean-to-lambdabox} is the Lean~4 frontend of Peregrine. The command
  \code{\#erase t config c to "f.ast"} elaborates the term \code{t}, erases it and the constants it
  depends on to a $\lambda_\Box$ program, and writes the S-expression \code{f.ast} that Peregrine
  (\code{peregrine-tool}) reads and compiles.
\item \lead{The object} \hl{the shipping eraser itself}, not an eraser written for the proof: the
  statements speak about the code that \code{\#erase} runs on the pure path.
\item \lead{The theorem} \code{erase\_correct} (Section~\ref{sec:statements-final}): if
  \code{\#erase} returns a program for an in-scope term and the erased term evaluates, the
  program's main term evaluates in $\lambda_\Box$ to an erasure of the value, and the value means
  what the term means in every con-leche model. It mirrors MetaRocq's \code{erase\_correct}.
\item \lead{The kernel theory} con-leche (Section~\ref{sec:intro-conleche}): a checker for Lean's
  declarations, proved sound by a set-theoretic model.
\item \lead{The scope} Chapter~\ref{chap:scope}: programs that con-leche accepts, that the
  eraser's fragment test admits, and whose marked input con-leche certifies.
\item \lead{The references} MetaRocq's erasure correctness and the two earlier verifications of
  this eraser (Section~\ref{sec:intro-stack}).
\end{itemize}

\section{The kernel model: con-leche}\label{sec:intro-conleche}

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{What it is} \code{github.com/leanprover/con-leche}, a checker for Lean's export format
  with its own expression type, pinned at commit \code{d88e04e} on Lean \code{v4.35.0}
  (Section~\ref{sec:intro-pins}). It has no Lake dependency and is never edited.
\item \lead{Main theorem} \leandecl{ConLeche.model_exists}: if the checker accepts a list of
  declarations, their environment has a model in every \code{[SetTheory V]}.
\item \lead{No typing judgement} con-leche has no syntactic typing relation and no subject
  reduction. Its theory is a set model (\leandecl{ConLeche.Denotes}) and an invariant on it; the
  relations that describe the checker's moves have no transitivity of conversion.
\item \lead{What it covers} Lean's whole kernel: inductive families (mutual, nested), recursors,
  $\iota$, projections, structure $\eta$, quotients, literals.
\item \lead{What it declines} user axioms, and \code{Nat.div} and \code{Nat.mod} declarations at
  the empty pin list.
\item \lead{The model hypothesis} the \code{SetTheory} class is instantiated only in con-leche's
  \code{bridge/} package, from an hypothesis on inaccessible cardinals; no module of this
  repository imports it (Section~\ref{sec:trust-conleche}).
\end{itemize}

\section{The stack}\label{sec:intro-stack}

This is the third verification of the pure path of \code{\#erase}, after lean4lean (PR \#13) and
Metalean (PR \#16). It shares their shipping code, and takes the proof tooling and the
kernel-agnostic parts of the Metalean development.

\begin{tabular}{p{0.3\linewidth}p{0.62\linewidth}}
\toprule
Part & Against the Metalean verification \\
\midrule
Kernel theory & con-leche's set model and checker, with no syntactic metatheory, in place of
  Metalean's typing judgement with subject reduction and uniqueness of types \\
Source semantics & marked terms and frozen evaluation (Chapter~\ref{chap:resolution}) in place of
  erasability by typing \\
Hypotheses & the certificate \code{hcert} replaces the typing premise of every $\Box$; user axioms
  and compiled pre-definitions are out of scope \\
Reused & the traversal and the pure path (shipping entries), $\lambda_\Box$ evaluation, the check
  scripts, the registers' formats and the tooling of this blueprint \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\section{Kinds, statuses and dependency graphs}\label{sec:intro-kinds}

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Kind} \code{blueprint/scripts/kinds.py} derives the kind of every node from its Lean
  declarations, its environment, and the \code{\textbackslash uses} of the final theorem and the
  milestones. The audit checks the derivation, and checks the \code{\textbackslash uses} of a
  formalized final theorem or milestone against its Lean proof. The kind sets the badge after the
  node's heading, the bar beside the node in the web version, and the node's shape and fill in the
  dependency graphs.
\item \lead{Colour families} Blue for the proof library \code{EraseProof}, orange for the shipping
  code, violet for the tests, green for con-leche; gold for the final theorem. Within a family the
  shape tells the kinds apart.
\item \lead{Status} The border of a graph node gives its status; a planned node also carries a
  second badge after its heading.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Graphs} The web version lays out three kinds of dependency graph when it is built:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item the chapter map: one box per chapter and one for con-leche, with their nodes by kind; an

    they use (on the left) and that use them (on the right), faded;

    uses.

  field above the graph finds a node by any of its Lean names. The link \emph{graph} in the
