\section{What is verified}\label{sec:intro-what}

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{The eraser} \code{lean-to-lambdabox} is the Lean~4 frontend of Peregrine. The command
  \code{\#erase t config c to "f.ast" mli "f.mli"} elaborates the term \code{t}, erases it and the
  constants it depends on to a $\lambda_\Box$ program, and writes three files:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \code{f.ast}: the program as an S-expression, which Peregrine (\code{peregrine-tool})
        reads and compiles;
  \item \code{f.ast.inlinings}: the constants Peregrine should inline;
  \item \code{f.mli}: an OCaml signature for the compiled program.
  \end{itemize}
  Section~\ref{sec:trust-shipping} describes the code.
\item \lead{The theorem} \code{erase\_correct} (Chapter~\ref{chap:final}): if \code{\#erase}
  returns a program for an in-scope term and the term evaluates, the program's main term
  evaluates in $\lambda_\Box$ to an erasure of the term's value. It mirrors MetaRocq's
  \code{erase\_correct}.
\item \lead{The scope} closed terms of the $\lambda\Pi$ fragment with \code{let} whose constants are
  axioms, definitions, theorems or opaques, well typed in lean4lean's model; no inductive types
  (Chapter~\ref{chap:scope}).
\item \lead{The references} the verification follows these as closely as the differences between
  Lean and Rocq allow; every divergence from the first two is an entry of
  \code{doc/DIVERGENCES.md}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Kinds, statuses and dependency graphs}\label{sec:intro-kinds}

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Kind} \code{blueprint/scripts/kinds.py} derives the kind of every node from its Lean
  declarations, its environment, and the \code{\textbackslash uses} of the final theorem and the
  milestones; the audit checks the derivation, and those \code{\textbackslash uses} against the
  Lean proofs. The kind sets the badge after the node's heading, the bar beside the node in the
  web version, and the node's shape and fill in the dependency graphs.
\item \lead{Colour families} Blue for the proof library \code{EraseProof}, orange for the shipping
  code, violet for the tests, green for lean4lean; gold for the final theorem. Within a family the
  shape tells the kinds apart.
\item \lead{Status} The border of a graph node gives its status, and so does a second badge after
  the heading of a node that inherits lean4lean sorries (with their labels) or is planned.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Graphs} The web version lays out three kinds of dependency graph when it is built:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item the chapter map: one box per chapter and one for lean4lean, with their nodes by kind; an
    arrow between two boxes carries the number of arrows between their nodes;
  \item the graph of a chapter, and that of lean4lean: its nodes, with the nodes outside it that
    they use (on the left) and that use them (on the right), faded;
  \item the graph of all nodes: a frame per chapter of the proof library, and one each for
    lean4lean (its modules and sorries), the shipping code and the tests, arranged top to bottom
    so that a frame sits below the frames it uses; the final theorem is at the bottom of the proof
    library, below the two milestones and their step lemmas.
  \end{itemize}
\item \lead{Navigation} A click on a node shows its statement, its arrows and its neighbours. The
  field above the graph finds a node by any of its Lean names. The link \emph{graph} in the
  heading of a node opens the graph of its chapter (or of lean4lean) at the node.
\end{itemize}
