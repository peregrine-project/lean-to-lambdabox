\chapter{The shipping eraser and its specification (ErasureSpec, EraserAsks, the oracle reroute)}
\label{chap:eraser}

Chapters~\ref{chap:lambdabox}--\ref{chap:lowering} describe \emph{relations}: source terms
related to \lbox{} terms, and \lbox{} terms lowered, optimised and observed. This chapter
describes the \emph{program}. The Lean~4 command \code{\#erase} calls \code{Erasure.erase},
which walks a \code{Lean.Expr} and returns an untyped \lbox{} program. The eraser is a
\code{CoreM} program: it reads a \code{Lean.Environment} --- an opaque primitive with a private
constructor --- through four queries, mints fresh \code{FVarId}s out of \code{IO.RealWorld},
and at every node asks an oracle whether the subterm is computationally irrelevant. None of that
is a mathematical object inside Lean, so none of it can be reasoned about directly. The chapter
therefore has three parts: the eraser itself, the \emph{oracle reroute} that turns its central
semantic decision from an assumption into a theorem, and the two hypothesis bundles ---
\code{ErasureSpec} and \code{EraserAsks} --- that write down, once and auditably, everything
else that is assumed about the world outside pure Lean terms.

The Lean files are \code{LeanToLambdaBox/Erasure.lean} (the eraser, roughly a thousand lines of
shipping code), \code{LeanToLambdaBox/Relevance.lean} and
\code{LeanToLambdaBox/RelevanceCheck.lean} (the verified relevance check and its soundness
proof), \code{LeanToLambdaBox/CheckerAdequacy.lean} (the adequacy of a checker run at an
arbitrary ambient local context), \code{LeanToLambdaBox/ErasureSpec.lean} (the two bundles and
what they prove), \code{LeanToLambdaBox/CasesNames.lean} and
\code{LeanToLambdaBox/Origin.lean} (reading a Lean constant three ways). A namespace warning
applies throughout: \code{Erasure.lean} opens \code{namespace Erasure} at the \emph{root}, so
every eraser declaration is \code{Erasure.foo} and not \code{LeanToLambdaBox.Erasure.foo}, while
\code{CheckerAdequacy.lean} deliberately declares seven names into the dependency's namespace
\code{Lean4Lean.TypeChecker}.

The eraser follows [S, Sec. 7.3] closely where it can: the first act of
\code{Erasure.visitExpr} at \emph{every} node is the relevance test, and if it succeeds the
whole subterm becomes $\Box$ with no recursion, which is MetaCoq's box rule in MetaCoq's own
place; the demand-driven closure over reachable constants and inductives is MetaCoq's
\code{erase\_global\_decls}. The oracle reroute is the one place the development goes beyond
MetaRocq's architecture: MetaRocq's \code{is\_erasable} runs on the kernel it is verified against
by construction, whereas the Lean eraser originally decided relevance with elaborator code that
has no specification, and the reroute replaces that with a chain \emph{proved} against
lean4lean's verified executable kernel checker. Two things must be said in the same breath as
every statement below. First, the verification is stated at a \emph{pinned} configuration, and
the default \code{\#erase} configuration differs from it on three of its five fields, so a bare
\code{\#erase foo to "foo.ast"} runs a configuration the theorems say nothing about. Second, two
of the five fields of \code{EraserAsks} are documented in the source as \emph{refuted in
general}, so every result that takes the bundle is bounded by them.

\section{The configuration, the state and the erasure monad}
\label{sec:eraser:config}

\begin{definition}[The erasure configuration]
  \label{def:Erasure-ErasureConfig}
  \lean{Erasure.ErasureConfig, Erasure.Config.Extern, Erasure.Config.Nat}
  \leanok
  The eraser is parameterised by five knobs. \code{extern} says whether a constant carrying
  Lean's \code{@[extern]} attribute is compiled from its logical Lean body
  (\code{.preferLogical}) or emitted as a body-less \lbox{} axiom to be realised on the target
  side (\code{.preferAxiom}). \code{nat} says whether \code{Nat} is compiled as the unary
  inductive (\code{.peano}) or as \lbox{} primitive 63-bit integers, with \code{Nat.zero},
  \code{Nat.succ} and \code{Nat.casesOn} rewritten into machine arithmetic (\code{.machine}).
  The Booleans \code{csimp}, \code{remove\_irrel\_constr\_args} and
  \code{auto\_inline\_typeclass\_dispatch} say whether \code{@[csimp]} replacements are applied
  before erasure, whether irrelevant constructor fields are pruned, and whether
  typeclass-dispatch artifacts are marked for inlining after erasure.

  The Lean structure \code{Erasure.ErasureConfig} fixes the defaults
  \code{extern := .preferAxiom}, \code{nat := .machine}, \code{csimp := true}, and \code{false}
  for the last two.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The pinned configuration]
  \label{def:ConfigPinned}
  \uses{def:Erasure-ErasureConfig}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.ConfigPinned}
  \leanok
  \code{ConfigPinned cfg} is the conjunction of five equations pinning every field of the
  configuration: \code{csimp = false}, \code{extern = .preferLogical}, \code{nat = .peano},
  \code{remove\_irrel\_constr\_args = false} and
  \code{auto\_inline\_typeclass\_dispatch = false}. Every correctness statement of this
  development is made at a configuration satisfying it.

  The Lean definition \code{ConfigPinned} is a plain \code{Prop}-valued conjunction; it occurs as
  the binder \code{hcfg} of the capstone and of every green rung, where it is discharged
  concretely by five reflexivities (Chapter~\ref{chap:capstone}).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
  Three of the five clauses pin a field \emph{away} from its Lean default. The pinned
  configuration is therefore not the one a bare \code{\#erase foo to "foo.ast"} runs; it must be
  asked for explicitly. The honest reading is that the machine-\code{Nat} layer, the
  \code{@[csimp]} layer and the \code{@[extern]}-axiom layer --- exactly the layers [R] describes
  as implemented --- are outside the verified perimeter by construction, and that this is
  \emph{stated} rather than silently omitted.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[The erasure monad]
  \label{def:Erasure-EraseM}
  \uses{def:Erasure-ErasureConfig, def:Kername, def:GlobalDeclarations, def:InductiveId}
  \lean{Erasure.EraseM, Erasure.ErasureState, Erasure.ErasureContext, Erasure.run, Erasure.liftMetaM}
  \leanok
  The eraser runs in \code{EraseM}, a state monad over a reader monad over \code{CoreM}. The
  state holds the memo table of registered inductives with their per-constructor field masks, the
  memo table mapping a Lean \code{Name} to the \lbox{} kername it was registered under, the
  accumulating \lbox{} global environment, and the kernames to hand the middle-end as inlining
  hints. The reader holds the Lean local context the subject is being erased in, an optional map
  sending each member of the mutual block currently being erased to the fresh free variable
  standing for it, the declaration-level universe parameters threaded to the relevance oracle,
  and the configuration.

  The Lean declarations are \code{Erasure.ErasureState}, \code{Erasure.ErasureContext} and
  \code{Erasure.EraseM}; \code{Erasure.run} starts an action from the empty state and the default
  context at a given configuration, and \code{Erasure.liftMetaM} runs a \code{MetaM} action in
  the reader's local context.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
  Because \code{CoreM} is an exception-and-state monad and the \code{Inhabited} default of
  \code{LBTerm} is $\Box$, a \code{panic!} or \code{unreachable!} inside \code{EraseM} does
  \emph{not} abort the run: it prints to standard error, yields $\Box$, and the command still
  exits successfully having written a wrong \code{.ast}. The eraser has sixteen such sites.
\end{remark}

\section{The relevance oracle and its soundness}
\label{sec:eraser:oracle}

\begin{definition}[The relevance criterion on the verified checker]
  \label{def:isErasable}
  \uses{def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.isErasable, LeanToLambdaBox.isErasableProp, LeanToLambdaBox.isArityCheck, LeanToLambdaBox.isArityCheck.loop}
  \leanok
  The erasability criterion of Letouzey and of [S, Sec. 7.3] --- a term is erasable when its type
  is a proposition, so the term is a proof, or an arity, so the term is a type or a type former
  --- re-implemented as an \emph{executable} check on lean4lean's verified kernel type checker
  rather than on Lean's elaborator. The proof branch infers the type of the subject with the
  kernel's \code{inferType} and asks the kernel's \code{isProp}. The arity branch
  weak-head-normalises the type, peels one \code{forall} binder at a time under a fresh local
  declaration, returns success at a sort and failure at any other head, and \textbf{throws} when
  its fuel runs out, so that the caller's error branch decides instead of a spurious negative.
  The whole oracle infers the type once and takes the disjunction.

  The Lean declarations are \code{LeanToLambdaBox.isErasableProp},
  \code{LeanToLambdaBox.isArityCheck.loop}, \code{LeanToLambdaBox.isArityCheck} and
  \code{LeanToLambdaBox.isErasable}; all four live in lean4lean's recursive checker monad, and
  \code{isArityCheck} starts the loop at the fuel \code{ty.approxDepth + 1}.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
  \code{Lean.Expr.Data.approxDepth} is an eight-bit field, so that fuel saturates at 256 and is
  \code{1} at a definitional alias, whose \code{approxDepth} is \code{0}. The measured
  consequence (finding F-DEPTH) is that on an inductive type former behind an
  \code{@[irreducible]} alias the kernel run throws, the eraser falls back to the elaborator
  oracle, and the answer is negative. This bounds the \code{EraserAsks} field
  \code{kernel\_ind\_head\_true} and, through it, Theorem~\ref{thm:EraserAsks-oracle_informative}.
  Because the exhaustion arm throws rather than answering \code{false}, F-DEPTH is a
  \emph{completeness} defect and not a soundness one. Making that arm throw is the single applied
  edit this verification effort made to code the eraser executes; every other entry of the
  finding register was reported and not fixed.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[The relevance decision the eraser makes]
  \label{def:Erasure-isErasable}
  \uses{def:isErasable, def:Erasure-EraseM}
  \lean{Erasure.isErasable, Erasure.isErasableMeta}
  \leanok
  The decision the erasure family actually makes. It runs the verified check of
  Definition~\ref{def:isErasable} \emph{purely}, through lean4lean's runner, in the ambient
  kernel environment, in the current local context, and at the caller-supplied universe
  parameters --- the declaration's own, threaded through the reader --- so that the checker runs
  in the universe scope the verification reasons about. On a successful run it returns the
  verdict. If the kernel checker errors, because of an unsupported construct or because the arity
  check's fuel was exhausted, it falls back to the elaborator-based check
  ``\code{Meta.isProp} or \code{Meta.isTypeFormerType}'', so that the transpiler never fails on
  that account.

  The Lean declarations are \code{Erasure.isErasable}, a \code{MetaM} function taking the
  universe parameters as its first argument, and \code{Erasure.isErasableMeta}, the fallback.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
  Two declarations in this development are called \code{isErasable} and they must never be
  confused: \code{LeanToLambdaBox.isErasable} (Definition~\ref{def:isErasable}) is the
  \emph{verified pure check}, and \code{Erasure.isErasable}
  (Definition~\ref{def:Erasure-isErasable}) is the \emph{impure decision the eraser calls}. The
  two arms of the latter have different statuses, and the blueprint keeps them apart: the kernel
  arm's soundness is a theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:Oracle-kernel_isErasable_sound}), the fallback
  arm's is an assumption (Definition~\ref{def:Oracle-MetaSound}, consumed by the
  \code{oracle\_meta} field). The source's doc comment ``no axiom of ours'' is defensible only
  under the narrow reading that this repository declares no \code{axiom} of its own: the proofs
  below do inherit \code{sorryAx} and lean4lean's modelling axioms. Empirically the error route is
  dead --- zero fallback hits over $139{,}196$ constants --- but that measurement concerns the
  \emph{error} route, not the scope-mismatch arm of \code{oracle\_meta}.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Oracle soundness, proof disjunct]
  \label{lem:isErasableProp-WF}
  \uses{def:isErasable, def:Erasable, def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.isErasableProp.WF}
  \leanok
  If \code{e} translates to the lean4lean term \code{e'} in the verified context, and the
  executable proof-branch check returns success on \code{e}, then \code{e'} is \Erasable{} by the
  proof disjunct: its inferred type itself lives in \code{Sort 0}.

  The Lean statement \code{isErasableProp.WF} is a Hoare-style obligation in lean4lean's
  well-formedness discipline: it quantifies over an arbitrary well-formed checker state and
  concludes about the returned Boolean, not about a particular run.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{asm:lean4lean-trust, asm:lean-reflection-axioms}
  Compose lean4lean's well-formedness lemma for \code{inferType}, which yields an inferred-type
  lean4lean term together with a typing and a translation witness, with the one for
  \code{isProp}, which turns a positive verdict on that type into a typing at \code{Sort 0}. That
  is exactly the first disjunct of \Erasable{}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Arity-up-to-definitional-equality is closed under forall-introduction]
  \label{lem:IsArityUpTo-forallE}
  \uses{def:IsArity, def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.IsArityUpTo.forallE}
  \leanok
  If the codomain \code{B} is an arity up to definitional equality in the extended context, and
  both \code{A} and \code{B} are types, then \code{forall (\_ : A), B} is an arity up to
  definitional equality.

  The Lean statement \code{IsArityUpTo.forallE} additionally carries the ambient hypotheses that
  the environment is well formed and that the context is a context of types. This is the one
  genuinely new metatheorem the arity branch of the reroute needs --- lean4lean does not have it
  --- and it is what lets the telescope recursion of Lemma~\ref{lem:isArityCheck-WF} reassemble a
  whole-type witness out of its body's.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{asm:lean4lean-trust}
  Unpack the witness that \code{B} is definitionally equal to a syntactic arity \code{Y}.
  Untyped congruence for \code{forall} lifts that equality to one between
  \code{forall A, B} and \code{forall A, Y}, and \code{forall A, Y} is syntactically an arity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Oracle soundness, type-former disjunct]
  \label{lem:isArityCheck-WF}
  \uses{def:isErasable, def:IsArity, def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.isArityCheck.loop.WF, LeanToLambdaBox.isArityCheck.WF}
  \leanok
  If \code{ty} translates to \code{ty'} and the executable arity loop returns success at any
  fuel, then \code{ty'} is an arity up to definitional equality; and likewise for the fuelled
  entry point, which starts the loop at \code{ty.approxDepth + 1}.

  The Lean statements \code{isArityCheck.loop.WF} and \code{isArityCheck.WF} are universally
  quantified over the fuel, so nothing about the fuel's adequacy is claimed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:IsArityUpTo-forallE, asm:lean4lean-trust, asm:lean-reflection-axioms}
  Induction on the fuel. After weak-head normalisation, the sort leaf gives the base arity: its
  translation is a sort definitionally equal to the reduced type. The \code{forall} node opens the
  binder with lean4lean's local-declaration combinator, applies the induction hypothesis under it,
  reassembles the whole-telescope witness with Lemma~\ref{lem:IsArityUpTo-forallE}, and transports
  it back across the reduction with the definitional-equality closure of the arity predicate. Fuel
  exhaustion throws, so the obligation is vacuous there --- which is precisely why the loop throws
  rather than answering negatively.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Relevance-oracle soundness]
  \label{thm:isErasable-WF}
  \uses{def:isErasable, def:Erasable, def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.isErasable.WF}
  \leanok
  If \code{e} translates to \code{e'} and the executable oracle returns success on \code{e}, then
  \code{e'} is \Erasable{}.

  The Lean statement \code{isErasable.WF} is again an obligation at an arbitrary well-formed
  checker state and context, so it applies to every run the checker can make, not to one. It is
  the theorem the shipping eraser's own doc comment points to, and its file's closing note is
  explicit that no new \code{axiom}, \code{sorry} or \code{native\_decide} is added here: the
  inherited footprint is lean4lean's (Chapter~\ref{chap:trust}).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:isErasableProp-WF, lem:isArityCheck-WF, asm:lean4lean-trust, asm:lean-reflection-axioms}
  The oracle infers the type once and branches. On the \code{isProp} branch the conclusion is the
  proof disjunct of \Erasable{}, by Lemma~\ref{lem:isErasableProp-WF}; on the other branch it is
  the type-former disjunct, by Lemma~\ref{lem:isArityCheck-WF}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[A verification context at an arbitrary ambient local context]
  \label{def:Lean4Lean-TypeChecker-VContext-ofMLCtx}
  \uses{def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{Lean4Lean.TypeChecker.VContext.ofMLCtx, Lean4Lean.TypeChecker.kernelNGen, Lean4Lean.TypeChecker.VContext.ofMLCtx_venv, Lean4Lean.TypeChecker.VContext.ofMLCtx_lparams, Lean4Lean.TypeChecker.VContext.ofMLCtx_vlctx}
  \leanok
  The kernel type checker's initial name generator has prefix \code{\_kernel\_fresh} and index
  zero; because its index is zero it \emph{reserves} every free variable not of that numbered
  shape, in particular every \code{\_uniq}-named identifier an actual \code{CoreM} run produces.
  On top of it, \code{VContext.ofMLCtx} builds a lean4lean verification context at an
  \textbf{arbitrary} ambient modelled local context, where lean4lean's own constructor hardwires
  the empty one; all other fields are taken over unchanged.

  The Lean declarations are \code{Lean4Lean.TypeChecker.kernelNGen} and
  \code{Lean4Lean.TypeChecker.VContext.ofMLCtx}, with three simp projections recording that the
  built context's environment, universe parameters and modelled local context are the given ones.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
  These five names, and the two of Lemma~\ref{lem:Lean4Lean-TypeChecker-M-WF-run-prime}, are
  declared into the \emph{dependency's} namespace \code{Lean4Lean.TypeChecker}: a knowing,
  documented deferral that the repository's own hygiene criterion records as a deliberate
  failure, pending an upstream ask. It is a real hazard --- a future lean4lean release declaring
  \code{M.WF.run'} would clash. The source calls the reservation premise ``vacuous''; strictly it
  is \emph{cheap}, not vacuous.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Run adequacy at the ambient local context]
  \label{lem:Lean4Lean-TypeChecker-M-WF-run-prime}
  \uses{def:Lean4Lean-TypeChecker-VContext-ofMLCtx, def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{Lean4Lean.TypeChecker.M.WF.run', Lean4Lean.TypeChecker.VState.WF.initial}
  \leanok
  The initial checker state, whose name generator is the kernel's own, is well formed at a
  context built by \code{VContext.ofMLCtx}, provided every ambient free variable is reserved by
  that generator; and consequently, if a checker computation satisfies its well-formedness
  obligation at that context, then a successful run of it at the ambient local context returns a
  value satisfying the postcondition. This is lean4lean's own run lemma generalised from the
  empty local context to an arbitrary ambient one.

  The Lean statements are \code{VState.WF.initial} and \code{M.WF.run'}; the latter carries the
  freshness premise as an explicit hypothesis over the modelled context's free variables.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{asm:lean-reflection-axioms}
  \code{VState.WF.initial} is lean4lean's empty-context state lemma with the empty modelled
  context replaced by the ambient one: the translation context becomes the generic one of the
  built context, the two reservation obligations become the freshness premise, and the
  extension-context witness is taken at the ambient modelled context with a reflexive lift and an
  empty equivalence manager. \code{M.WF.run'} is then lean4lean's own run proof with the
  initial-state witness replaced by that one.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The kernel arm of the oracle is sound]
  \label{thm:Oracle-kernel_isErasable_sound}
  \uses{def:isErasable, def:Lean4Lean-TypeChecker-VContext-ofMLCtx, def:Erasable, def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.Oracle.kernel_isErasable_sound}
  \leanok
  A \emph{pure} run of the verified relevance check through lean4lean's runner, at the ambient
  local context and the declaration's universe parameters, returning success on a term \code{e}
  that translates to \code{ve}, witnesses that \code{ve} is \Erasable{}.

  The Lean statement \code{Oracle.kernel\_isErasable\_sound} takes the environment model's
  well-formedness, the modelled context's well-formedness and the reservation premise as
  hypotheses, and phrases the run as an equation on the runner's result rather than as an
  obligation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:isErasable-WF, lem:Lean4Lean-TypeChecker-M-WF-run-prime, asm:lean4lean-trust, asm:lean-reflection-axioms}
  A three-way composition, and the Lean proof is literally three lines:
  Theorem~\ref{thm:isErasable-WF} supplies soundness at the built context, lean4lean's
  well-formedness lemma for the recursive monad's runner discharges the fuel layer, and
  Lemma~\ref{lem:Lean4Lean-TypeChecker-M-WF-run-prime} converts the obligation into a statement
  about the successful run.
\end{proof}

\section{Preparation, registration, binders}
\label{sec:eraser:prepare}

\begin{definition}[Pre-erasure preparation]
  \label{def:Erasure-prepare_erasure}
  \uses{def:Erasure-EraseM, def:Erasure-ErasureConfig}
  \lean{Erasure.prepare_erasure, Erasure.replaceUnsafeRecNames, Erasure.remove_unsafe_rec, Erasure.name_occurs}
  \leanok
  Before erasure, the subject is normalised by a pass lifted from Lean's own compiler frontend:
  replace every \code{\_unsafe\_rec} constant by its safe twin, honour \code{@[macro\_inline]},
  inline auxiliary matchers, honour \code{@[macro\_inline]} a second time --- inlined matchers can
  expose \code{ite} and \code{dite}, whose branches would otherwise both be evaluated strictly ---
  and, only when \code{csimp} is on, apply \code{@[csimp]} replacements at every subterm.

  The Lean declarations are \code{Erasure.prepare\_erasure}, the first pass
  \code{Erasure.replaceUnsafeRecNames}, the name surgery \code{Erasure.remove\_unsafe\_rec}, and
  \code{Erasure.name\_occurs}, the eraser's only recursiveness test --- a structural occurrence
  check that treats \code{forall}, sorts, literals and variables as non-occurrences.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
  This pass has no MetaCoq counterpart, and its own doc comment warns that it ``may make the
  expression ill-typed if some dependent type relies on the implementation of functions affected
  by csimp''. \code{ConfigPinned} pins \code{csimp = false}, so the \code{@[csimp]} walk is
  outside the theorems' scope. \code{remove\_unsafe\_rec} is the site of finding F-UNSAFEREC: it
  is not injective, so a legal \code{mutual unsafe def u / u.\_unsafe\_rec} block maps to a key
  list with a repetition and is silently miscompiled. That is exactly what the \code{EraserAsks}
  field \code{block\_keys\_distinct} excludes by assumption.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[The prepared passes]
  \label{def:preparePasses}
  \uses{def:Erasure-prepare_erasure}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.preparePasses}
  \leanok
  The list of the three \code{CoreM} passes the preparation runs:
  \code{Erasure.replaceUnsafeRecNames}, Lean's \code{macroInline} and Lean's
  \code{inlineMatchers}.

  The Lean definition \code{preparePasses} is the \emph{index set} of the two pass clauses of
  \code{EraserAsks}, not the literal call sequence: the eraser makes four calls, running
  \code{macroInline} twice, and the quantification over membership in the list covers the
  repetition. A reader who counts three here and four in the code has counted correctly.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Registering an inductive block]
  \label{def:Erasure-register_inductive}
  \uses{def:Erasure-EraseM, def:Erasure-isErasable, def:Kername, def:InductiveId, def:OneInductiveBody}
  \lean{Erasure.register_inductive, Erasure.addAxiom, Erasure.ConstructorArgMask, Erasure.InductiveArgMasks, Erasure.ConstructorArgRelevance, Erasure.filter}
  \leanok
  Memoised registration of a Lean inductive together with all its mutual companions. On a miss
  the eraser synthesises the block's kername as the unqualified kername of the concatenation of
  all member names; walks each member's constructors, emitting a body-less axiom for an
  \code{@[extern]} constructor when the configuration prefers axioms and computing a
  per-constructor argument mask --- all fields kept unless \code{remove\_irrel\_constr\_args} is
  on, in which case each field is tested with the relevance oracle under the constructor's own
  universe parameters --- recording the post-pruning arity; emits dummy projection names for the
  kept fields of a single-constructor, single-member, non-recursive block; and conses one
  inductive declaration for the whole block onto the emitted environment, carrying the block's
  parameter count.

  The Lean declarations are \code{Erasure.register\_inductive}, the mask types
  \code{Erasure.ConstructorArgRelevance}, \code{Erasure.ConstructorArgMask} and
  \code{Erasure.InductiveArgMasks}, the mask application \code{Erasure.filter}, and
  \code{Erasure.addAxiom}, which registers a name with a body-less constant declaration and
  panics on a duplicate.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
  Finding F-PROP lives here: every emitted inductive body leaves \code{propositional} and
  \code{kelim} at their defaults and every mutual body leaves \code{finite} at its default, so the
  middle-end's match-on-box optimisation can never fire on this frontend's output. A second,
  unregistered defect is adjacent: \code{Erasure.filter} zips a mask against an array and drops
  trailing elements, so a mask shorter than the field array silently truncates. The \lbox{}
  identifier this function mints is the one \code{BlockAdequate}
  (Definition~\ref{def:BlockAdequate}) pins to the model's inductive data.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Emitting binders]
  \label{def:Erasure-mkLambda}
  \uses{def:Erasure-EraseM, def:LBTerm, def:toBvar, def:BinderName, def:FixDef}
  \lean{Erasure.mkLambda, Erasure.mkLetIn, Erasure.mkAlt, Erasure.mkDef, Erasure.fvar_to_name, Erasure.withLocalDecl, Erasure.withLocalDef, Erasure.lambdaMonocular, Erasure.lambdaOrIntroToArity}
  \leanok
  The eraser works locally namelessly: it descends under a binder by minting a fresh free
  variable and extending the reader's local context, and it closes a binder by naming that
  variable and abstracting it back to a de Bruijn index. Naming reads the user-facing name out of
  the local context and \textbf{downgrades it to anonymous unless every character is
  ASCII-graphic}, because the middle-end's parser rejects other binder names.

  The Lean declarations are \code{Erasure.withLocalDecl} and \code{Erasure.withLocalDef} for the
  descent, \code{Erasure.fvar\_to\_name} for the ASCII filter, \code{Erasure.mkLambda} and
  \code{Erasure.mkLetIn} for one binder, \code{Erasure.mkAlt} for a case alternative (binding the
  \emph{last} of its field variables to index zero), \code{Erasure.mkDef} for one member of a
  mutual fixpoint block (abstracting the block's fixpoint variables, principal-argument index left
  at zero), and the destructuring helpers \code{Erasure.lambdaMonocular} and
  \code{Erasure.lambdaOrIntroToArity}.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
  The ASCII filter is the source of the printable-binder clause of the emitted program's
  well-formedness predicate (Chapter~\ref{chap:lowering}); Chapter~\ref{chap:capstone} records
  that the stronger all-ASCII form was \emph{refuted} at five rungs. Two doc comments in the
  shipping source still hedge about binder order (``Check binding order here as well, may be
  wrong''); the question is settled by Chapters~\ref{chap:lowering} and~\ref{chap:refinement},
  but the hedge remains in the code.
\end{remark}

\section{The erasure family and the entry point}
\label{sec:eraser:visit}

\begin{definition}[The erasure family]
  \label{def:Erasure-visitExpr}
  \uses{def:Erasure-EraseM, def:Erasure-isErasable, def:Erasure-register_inductive, def:Erasure-mkLambda, def:LBTerm}
  \lean{Erasure.visitExpr, Erasure.visitLiteral, Erasure.visitLambda, Erasure.visitLet, Erasure.visitProj, Erasure.visitApp, Erasure.visitConst, Erasure.visitConstApp, Erasure.visitCasesEta, Erasure.visitCasesEtaGo, Erasure.visitCtorEta, Erasure.visitCtorEtaGo, Erasure.visitConstructor, Erasure.visitAppArgs, Erasure.visitCases, Erasure.visitAlt}
  \leanok
  Erasure proper is one mutual block of sixteen term-level definitions, each made total by
  \code{partial\_fixpoint}. Its head, \code{visitExpr}, does the box test first at \emph{every}
  node: if the oracle says the subterm is a proof or a type former, the result is $\Box$ and
  there is no recursion. Otherwise it dispatches on the head. Applications and bare constants both
  go through the application case --- a constant is treated as an application to zero arguments,
  so the special cases for eliminators and constructors fire uniformly. Projections re-index the
  field past the pruned ones and emit a \lbox{} projection. Metadata is skipped transparently.
  Lambdas and \code{let}s emit their binders through Definition~\ref{def:Erasure-mkLambda}. A
  literal is expanded into a unary \code{Nat.succ} chain under \code{nat = .peano} and into a
  primitive 63-bit integer under \code{.machine}. A free variable is emitted as an \lbox{} free
  variable, to be abstracted later. An eliminator or constructor that is under-applied is
  eta-expanded to its arity before the eliminator or constructor case runs. Four heads are
  declared unreachable: sorts and products, because they are always erasable and were boxed
  above; metavariables, because the supported fragment excludes them; and de Bruijn variables, by
  the locally nameless invariant.

  The Lean block is headed by \code{Erasure.visitExpr}; the other fifteen members are the
  dispatch targets named above.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
  The family was originally a single \code{partial def}, which produces an opaque constant with
  no equations. \code{partial\_fixpoint} produces equational lemmas and, crucially, the
  eighteen-motive fixpoint-induction principle \code{Erasure.visitExpr.mutual\_fixpoint\_induct}
  on which the whole refinement of Chapter~\ref{chap:refinement} and the cold start of
  Chapter~\ref{chap:coldstart} run. The cost is paid twice: a monotonicity section of nine
  proof-only lemmas teaching \code{partial\_fixpoint} how recursion flows through the
  continuation-passing helpers, which change the compiled behaviour of nothing; and two
  documented behaviour-preserving rewrites, the specialisation of a former combined eta helper
  (which survives as dead code) into the two eta loops, and one array conversion in the
  eliminator case.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
  The constructor case emits \emph{applied} form --- a bare constructor node applied through
  \lbox{} application --- never a block with its arguments inside; the inline comment says ``in
  the stage of lambda-box I am targeting constructor application is function application''. This
  is the opposite of agda2lambox's choice and of MetaRocq's \code{constructors\_as\_blocks}
  target, and it is why the semantics of Chapter~\ref{chap:lambdabox} is pinned with the
  block-constructor flag off (design tag F-ETA). [R]'s Listing~2 shows block form, so the report
  and the shipping code disagree on the record; the code wins. Two further findings sit at these
  sites: F-SPARSE, where the eliminator case recovers the inductive from the eliminator's name
  prefix and declares a sparse eliminator unreachable, after which the run continues and writes a
  wrong \code{.ast}; and F-ETA2, where the eta loops re-erase the supplied argument prefix once
  per missing argument, which in applied form buys nothing.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[The environment closure]
  \label{def:Erasure-visitMutual}
  \uses{def:Erasure-visitExpr, def:Erasure-prepare_erasure, def:Erasure-mkLambda, def:Erasure-EraseM, def:Kername, def:GlobalDeclarations}
  \lean{Erasure.visitMutual, Erasure.get_constant_kername}
  \leanok
  The two remaining members of the mutual block close the emitted environment over what the
  subject reaches. The kername lookup is memoised and, on a miss, erases the constant's whole Lean
  mutual block and re-reads the table. Erasing a block reads it through the compiler's declaration
  info --- the \emph{original} recursive definition where one exists, rather than the
  recursor-based elaboration --- and then either emits a body-less axiom (no compiler value, or
  \code{@[extern]} under the axiom policy); or, for a single declaration whose body does not
  mention its own name, erases the prepared body and conses a constant declaration; or otherwise
  mints one fresh free variable per block member, installs them in the reader keyed by the
  members' \code{\_unsafe\_rec}-stripped names, erases each prepared body under that map, closes
  each with the fixpoint-member constructor, and registers \emph{each} member under its own
  kername with body a bare, unapplied fixpoint block selected at that member's index.

  The Lean declarations are \code{Erasure.get\_constant\_kername} and
  \code{Erasure.visitMutual}.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
  This is MetaCoq's \code{erase\_global\_decls}, done on demand. It is the site of finding F-ETA:
  the emitted fixpoint bodies are bare and unapplied, so MetaRocq's expanded-program predicate is
  false on all five benchmark programs --- which is exactly why the capstone concludes the
  peregrine well-formedness predicate and pointedly not the expanded form. The eraser's own
  \code{TODO} sits in this branch. It is also the site of F-UNSAFEREC, since the fixpoint-variable
  map is keyed by stripped names, and of the body-less arm that produces findings F-QUOT and
  F-EQREC.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[The entry point]
  \label{def:Erasure-erase}
  \uses{def:Erasure-visitExpr, def:Erasure-prepare_erasure, def:Erasure-EraseM, def:Erasure-ErasureConfig, def:ASTType}
  \lean{Erasure.erase}
  \leanok
  The shipping eraser: prepare the subject, erase it from the empty state at the given
  configuration, and return the untyped \lbox{} program consisting of the accumulated global
  environment and the erased main term, together with the list of kernames to hint for inlining.

  The Lean definition \code{Erasure.erase} is a \code{CoreM} function returning a pair of the
  program and that inlining list; it is the function the capstone of
  Chapter~\ref{chap:capstone} is about.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
  Two boundaries must be stated here. First, the \code{\#erase} command wraps this function:
  it evaluates the user's configuration term with an \code{unsafe} evaluation, serialises the
  result and writes the \code{.ast} file --- and the S-expression printer \code{LBTerm.to\_sexpr}
  is a \code{partial def} that \textbf{nothing in the verification mentions}. The theorem stack
  therefore stops at the \code{Program} value: a claim ``the emitted \code{.ast} satisfies
  \ldots'' must be read as ``the emitted \code{Program} satisfies \ldots''. Second, the default
  configuration is not the pinned one (Definition~\ref{def:ConfigPinned}).
\end{remark}

\section{What the correctness statement assumes: ErasureSpec}
\label{sec:eraser:spec}

\begin{definition}[Soundness of a relevance verdict]
  \label{def:Oracle-MetaSound}
  \uses{def:Lean4Lean-TypeChecker-VContext-ofMLCtx, def:Erasable, def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.Oracle.MetaSound}
  \leanok
  \code{Oracle.MetaSound} says that a positive verdict on a term is sound at the ambient universe
  scope and at \emph{any} modelled local context whose underlying Lean local context is the given
  one and whose free variables are reserved by the kernel's initial name generator: every
  lean4lean translation of the term there is \Erasable{}. This is the conclusion the elaborator
  fallback is \emph{assumed} to deliver and the one the kernel arm \emph{proves}.

  The Lean definition \code{Oracle.MetaSound} is the shape in which the assumed arm and the
  proved arm meet in Theorem~\ref{thm:ErasureSpec-oracle_sound_of_run}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The environment queries report what the environment holds]
  \label{def:LookupAdequate}
  \uses{def:isCasesOnName, def:Erasure-prepare_erasure}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.LookupAdequate, LeanToLambdaBox.CasesInfoAgreesK, LeanToLambdaBox.altNumFields}
  \leanok
  The four environment queries the eraser makes --- for a constant's info, for the compiler's
  declaration info, for a constructor's arity, and for an eliminator's metadata --- report what
  the elaboration environment holds, and none does more to the fresh-name generator than advance
  it. Three clauses are deliberately asymmetric. The declaration-info clause's block-membership
  conclusion is \emph{guarded} by the name not being an \code{\_unsafe\_rec} name, because the
  primitive prefers the \code{\_unsafe\_rec} twin and at such a name the block it answers with
  does not contain the name asked for. The constructor-arity and eliminator-metadata clauses are
  stated in both directions; the negative directions are what the fragment predicate spends to
  rule out non-constructor and non-eliminator heads.

  The Lean declarations are the four-field structure \code{LookupAdequate}, the field-count
  helper \code{altNumFields}, and \code{CasesInfoAgreesK}, the payload of the last clause: the
  elaborator's \code{casesOn} metadata agrees with the declared inductive block --- the
  discriminant sits immediately after parameters, motive and indices, the eliminator is saturated
  by one minor premise per constructor, the alternatives run from one past the discriminant to
  the arity, there is one alternative per constructor, and each binds exactly its constructor's
  fields.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
  These are facts about opaque primitives. \code{Lean.Environment} has a private constructor, so
  no Lean term denotes one and no statement about \code{getConstInfo} is provable inside Lean.
  That is why the whole bundle is an assumption and not a lemma.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[The kernel's blocks in the model]
  \label{def:BlockAdequate}
  \uses{def:Erasure-register_inductive, def:isCasesOnName, def:IndInfo, def:IndArity, def:CtorOf, def:ConstOrigin, def:CasesOnShape}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.BlockAdequate, LeanToLambdaBox.KernelFields}
  \leanok
  The kernel's inductive blocks, constructors and eliminators are the model's, at the \lbox{}
  identifier the eraser mints. Forward: a member of a declared block is the model's type former at
  the block identifier built from the block's member names and at the member's position, with the
  block's parameter count and per-constructor field counts. Backward: a model type former is
  declared with the same arithmetic. Constructors correspond in both directions, at their
  inductive and index. The \code{casesOn} constant of a declared inductive is a declared plain
  constant of the model whose segmentation is exactly the block's --- the same arithmetic
  \code{CasesInfoAgreesK} reads off the elaborator's metadata. And a declared inductive is a
  member of its own mutual block.

  The Lean declarations are the six-field structure \code{BlockAdequate} and the field-count list
  \code{KernelFields}, one entry per constructor with that constructor agreeing about its
  inductive, its index and the block's parameter count. The field is spelled \code{casesOnDecl}
  rather than \code{casesOn}, because a Lean structure may not carry a field of the latter name.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The effectful calls only advance the generator]
  \label{def:PrimMonotone}
  \uses{def:Erasure-EraseM}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.PrimMonotone, LeanToLambdaBox.ForallMatchesLam}
  \leanok
  The four \code{CoreM} and \code{MetaM} calls the eraser makes for their effect alone ---
  reading the environment, logging, asking whether a constant is an instance, and inferring a type
  --- only advance the fresh-name generator; and type inference additionally reports a
  \code{forall}-telescope whose binders agree, in name and domain, with the subject's
  \code{lambda}-telescope.

  The Lean declarations are the four-field structure \code{PrimMonotone} and the telescope
  agreement \code{ForallMatchesLam}. The latter is \emph{vacuously true} whenever the subject is
  not a lambda, by design, so the inference clause asserts nothing about the inferred type of a
  constant or an application.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The specification of the eraser's primitives]
  \label{def:ErasureSpec}
  \uses{def:LookupAdequate, def:PrimMonotone, def:BlockAdequate, def:Oracle-MetaSound, def:isErasable, def:Erasure-isErasable, def:lean4lean-grounding, asm:ErasureSpec-env_connect, asm:ErasureSpec-lookup_adequate, asm:ErasureSpec-fresh_names, asm:ErasureSpec-oracle_refl, asm:ErasureSpec-oracle_meta, asm:ErasureSpec-decl_adequate, asm:ErasureSpec-prim_monotone, asm:ErasureSpec-block_adequate}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.ErasureSpec}
  \leanok
  \code{ErasureSpec} bundles eight clauses about the world outside pure Lean terms, at an
  elaboration environment, its lean4lean model, the ambient universe scope and a reading of the
  fresh-name generator.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{env\_connect}: there is a lean4lean environment model, well formed for the kernel
      environment obtained from the elaboration environment, whose safe projection is the model
      --- the same conversion the shipping oracle itself makes.
    \item \emph{lookup\_adequate}: Definition~\ref{def:LookupAdequate}.
    \item \emph{fresh\_names}: minting a fresh free variable returns an identifier the ambient
      generator has not handed out, reserves it afterwards, only advances the generator, and is
      reserved by the kernel's own initial generator.
    \item \emph{oracle\_refl}: a positive verdict of the shipping oracle at the ambient scope
      either reflects a successful \emph{pure} run of the verified checker at the same local
      context and scope, or is a verdict of the elaborator fallback, assumed sound.
    \item \emph{oracle\_meta}: a positive verdict at a scope other than the ambient one is sound
      outright.
    \item \emph{decl\_adequate}: a declaration the environment makes visible at safety
      \code{.safe} is visible in the model with a translated type.
    \item \emph{prim\_monotone}: Definition~\ref{def:PrimMonotone}.
    \item \emph{block\_adequate}: Definition~\ref{def:BlockAdequate}.
  \end{enumerate}
  The Lean declaration \code{ErasureSpec} is a \code{Prop}-valued structure with exactly those
  eight fields. It is the top hypothesis of the capstone, of every green rung, of the erasure
  bridge, of the fragment's soundness lemmas and of the cold-start induction.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
  This node is a \emph{definition}, not an assumption: the structure exists and elaborates. What
  is assumed is each of its eight \emph{fields}, and those are eight assumption nodes of
  Chapter~\ref{chap:trust} (trust class~D). Two readings deserve spelling out. The safety
  condition of \code{decl\_adequate} is written as an inequality with \code{.safe} on the left;
  since \code{.safe} is the \emph{top} of lean4lean's order this forces the declaration to be
  safe and does \textbf{not} admit unsafe or partial declarations, although the inequality reads
  the other way at first sight. And the bundle deliberately does \emph{not} carry the source-table
  adequacy hypothesis of Chapter~\ref{chap:capstone}: a \code{Prop}-valued structure cannot hold a
  table as data, and an unbound table field would auto-bind to a universal quantifier no table
  satisfies.
\end{remark}

\section{What the eraser is asked for: EraserAsks}
\label{sec:eraser:asks}

\begin{definition}[What the eraser is asked for]
  \label{def:EraserAsks}
  \uses{def:preparePasses, def:Erasure-isErasable, def:isErasable, def:Erasure-prepare_erasure, def:SEval, def:IndInfo, def:Kername, asm:EraserAsks-passes_monotone, asm:EraserAsks-passes_sound, asm:EraserAsks-oracle_false_refl, asm:EraserAsks-kernel_ind_head_true, asm:EraserAsks-block_keys_distinct}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.EraserAsks}
  \leanok
  \code{EraserAsks} bundles five clauses about \emph{this repository's own} code --- the
  preparation passes and the relevance oracle --- so that each is an obligation with an owner
  rather than a fact about an opaque object.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{passes\_monotone}: the preparation calls only advance the generator.
    \item \emph{passes\_sound}: each pass preserves the source evaluation of the subject
      \textbf{under an arbitrary application spine}. The capstone observes the program applied to
      arguments, while a pass is a whole-tree walk, and applying a pass to an application is not
      applying it to the head; a clause stated at the subject alone would not reach the spine.
    \item \emph{oracle\_false\_refl}: a negative verdict of the shipping oracle means the pure
      kernel run did not answer positively.
    \item \emph{kernel\_ind\_head\_true}: at an inductive-type head the pure kernel run answers
      positively --- the oracle's \emph{completeness} at the one shape the fragment predicate
      cannot exclude.
    \item \emph{block\_keys\_distinct}: the \lbox{} kernames of a mutual block's members are
      distinct.
  \end{enumerate}
  The Lean declaration \code{EraserAsks} is a \code{Prop}-valued structure with exactly those
  \textbf{five} fields; \code{EraserAsks.oracle\_informative} is not a sixth field but a derived
  theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:EraserAsks-oracle_informative}). The five fields are five assumption
  nodes of Chapter~\ref{chap:trust} (trust class~C).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
  Two of the five fields are documented \emph{refuted in general}. The completeness field is
  refuted by F-DEPTH: a telescope deeper than the capped fuel, or an \code{@[irreducible]} alias,
  defeats the arity check. The distinctness field is refuted by F-UNSAFEREC: a legal
  \code{mutual unsafe def u / u.\_unsafe\_rec} block collapses two kernames, which is a
  \emph{miscompilation} and not merely a proof gap; the source records a proposed one-line
  duplicate guard. Both were measured on a large corpus and held there --- $2{,}940$ of
  $2{,}940$ inductive type formers, and no failure over more than two hundred thousand
  declaration-info answers. Because the bundle is a binder of the capstone and of all eight rungs,
  those statements are conditionally vacuous on any environment exhibiting a counterexample. The
  repository's own status document says ``four fields'' and then lists five; the code has five.
\end{remark}

\section{What the bundles prove}
\label{sec:eraser:derived}

\begin{theorem}[The modelled environment is well formed]
  \label{thm:ErasureSpec-envWF}
  \uses{def:ErasureSpec, def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.ErasureSpec.envWF}
  \leanok
  Under the specification bundle, the model environment is well formed.

  The Lean statement \code{ErasureSpec.envWF} takes the bundle as its only hypothesis. This is a
  genuine reduction rather than an assumption, and a load-bearing one: well-formedness of the
  model environment is the premise of essentially every target-side lemma of
  Chapters~\ref{chap:erases}--\ref{chap:lowering}, and it is \emph{derived} from the model
  connection instead of assumed separately. Its measured footprint is the standard one.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{asm:ErasureSpec-env_connect}
  Unpack the model-connection field and apply lean4lean's well-formedness of a translated
  environment at safety \code{.safe}. Two lines.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Declaration adequacy, for the kernel's own lookup]
  \label{prop:ErasureSpec-decl_adequate_of_kernelFind}
  \uses{def:ErasureSpec, def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.ErasureSpec.decl_adequate_of_kernelFind}
  \leanok
  The content of the \code{decl\_adequate} field, proved --- but for the \emph{kernel}
  environment's own lookup rather than the elaborator's: a declaration the kernel environment
  makes visible at safety \code{.safe} is visible in the model with a translated type.

  The Lean statement \code{ErasureSpec.decl\_adequate\_of\_kernelFind} differs from the field it
  mirrors exactly in which of the two lookup primitives it quantifies over. It is
  documentation-by-theorem of how far the field can be reduced and of what blocks the rest ---
  the agreement of the elaborator's lookup with the kernel's, two opaque primitives, an upstream
  ask. It is proved but \textbf{never applied}; a reader must not mistake it for a discharge of
  the field.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{asm:ErasureSpec-env_connect, asm:lean-reflection-axioms}
  The model-connection field yields lean4lean's translated-environment predicate, whose own lookup
  lemma reads a visible declaration off it.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The oracle reroute]
  \label{thm:ErasureSpec-oracle_sound_of_run}
  \uses{def:ErasureSpec, def:Erasure-isErasable, def:Oracle-MetaSound, def:Erasable, def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.ErasureSpec.oracle_sound_of_run}
  \leanok
  If the shipping relevance oracle returns a positive verdict on a term during a run, at the
  ambient universe scope, then every lean4lean translation of that term --- at a modelled local
  context whose underlying Lean local context is the run's and whose free variables are reserved
  by the kernel's initial generator --- is \Erasable{}, hence a legitimate target of the box rule.

  The Lean statement \code{ErasureSpec.oracle\_sound\_of\_run} phrases the verdict as an equation
  on a concrete lifted \code{MetaM} run, with the run's world token and state explicit, rather
  than as an abstract Boolean.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:Oracle-kernel_isErasable_sound, def:Oracle-MetaSound, asm:ErasureSpec-env_connect, asm:ErasureSpec-oracle_refl, asm:lean4lean-trust, asm:lean-reflection-axioms}
  Unpack the model connection. The \code{oracle\_refl} field offers two disjuncts. On the kernel
  disjunct --- a successful pure run of the verified checker at the same local context and scope
  --- apply Theorem~\ref{thm:Oracle-kernel_isErasable_sound}. On the fallback disjunct the
  conclusion \emph{is} \code{Oracle.MetaSound} applied to the witnesses at hand.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  This is the single place where the erasure's relevance decision stops being an assumption and
  becomes a consequence of a verified executable checker. What remains assumed on this path is
  only the \emph{reflection} step --- that the impure \code{MetaM} plumbing reflects the pure run
  it calls --- and the fallback arm. It is the first step of the erasure bridge, so its footprint
  propagates to the capstone and to all eight rungs. That footprint is measured, in the committed
  fixture \code{test/ledger.expected}: 33 axiom names, namely \code{propext}, \code{sorryAx},
  \code{Classical.choice} and \code{Quot.sound} together with 29 non-standard names --- lean4lean's
  pointer-equality axioms, its modelling axioms for native \code{Expr}, \code{Level} and the
  persistent containers, one for \code{Std.TreeMap}, one for syntax, and two Lean-core
  \code{bv\_decide} certificates. The repository records the alternative on the table: revert the
  reroute, about forty lines, and keep the oracle sound by assumption.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[The type-former exclusion, derived]
  \label{thm:EraserAsks-oracle_informative}
  \uses{def:EraserAsks, def:Erasure-isErasable, def:IndInfo, def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.EraserAsks.oracle_informative}
  \leanok
  If the shipping oracle answers negatively on a term at the ambient universe scope, then the head
  of that term's application spine is not an inductive type name.

  The Lean statement \code{EraserAsks.oracle\_informative} carries the same run-level equation and
  the same modelled-context premises as
  Theorem~\ref{thm:ErasureSpec-oracle_sound_of_run}, and concludes for every constant head and
  every universe instantiation at once.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{asm:EraserAsks-oracle_false_refl, asm:EraserAsks-kernel_ind_head_true}
  The two oracle clauses of the bundle contradict each other at an inductive head: the
  completeness field says the pure kernel run answers positively there, while the reflection field
  says it did not answer positively.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  This is a theorem, not a field of \code{EraserAsks}. It is the module's advertised template for
  retiring the remaining fields: derive an \code{EraserAsks}-shaped fact from two weaker clauses
  instead of assuming it. It tells a \emph{relevant} position of the erasure relation that it is
  not looking at a type former, and Chapter~\ref{chap:refinement} consumes it. Its truth is
  conditional on the bundle, and one of the two fields it spends is refuted in general, so it is
  exactly as strong as \code{kernel\_ind\_head\_true} and no stronger.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[An inductive-type-name spine is erasable]
  \label{lem:erasable_indSpine}
  \uses{def:IndInfo, def:Erasable, def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.erasable_indSpine}
  \leanok
  If the model knows a constant as an inductive type former, then every application of it to
  arguments that translates is \Erasable{}.

  The Lean statement \code{erasable\_indSpine} additionally requires the environment and the
  modelled local context to be well formed, and quantifies over all argument lists at once. It is
  the positive half of the ``type formers are erased'' discipline --- the statement that the box
  rule \emph{does} cover an applied type constructor --- but it has no consumer anywhere in the
  repository, and a design document places its intended home in a different module. It is recorded
  here, where the code puts it, and it is not load-bearing.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:Erases-indInfo_erasable, thm:Erasable-app, asm:lean4lean-trust}
  Strong induction on the spine length, peeling the last argument. The head's type is an arity, so
  the head is erasable, and erasability is carried one argument at a time along the spine.
\end{proof}

\section{Reading a constant three ways}
\label{sec:eraser:origin}

\begin{definition}[Recognising an eliminator by its name]
  \label{def:isCasesOnName}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.isCasesOnName, LeanToLambdaBox.lastComponent}
  \leanok
  A constant is treated as an eliminator exactly when its last name component is the string
  \code{casesOn}.

  The Lean declarations are \code{lastComponent}, which returns the last string component of a
  hierarchical name if it has one, and \code{isCasesOnName}, which compares it. The criterion is
  deliberately \emph{syntactic}: the development never consults a kernel ``is this a recursor''
  flag, which is why the module is a leaf depending only on Lean's name datatype. It is read by
  the source-side eliminator segmentation predicate of Chapter~\ref{chap:source}, by the fragment
  checker, and by two clauses of \code{ErasureSpec}.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Every declared constant is a constructor, a type name or a plain constant]
  \label{lem:consts_classified}
  \uses{def:UpstreamAsks, def:CtorOf, def:IndInfo, def:ConstOrigin, def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.consts_classified, LeanToLambdaBox.constOrigin_not_ctorOf, LeanToLambdaBox.constOrigin_not_indInfo, LeanToLambdaBox.constOrigin_of_constants}
  \leanok
  Lean has one constant node where Rocq has three, so the three readings of a constant occurrence
  --- a constructor, an inductive type name, a plain constant --- must be recovered from the
  environment. Under the upstream ask bundle: every declared constant of the model admits one of
  the three readings; a plain constant is neither a constructor nor an inductive type name; and
  conversely a declared constant that is neither is a plain constant.

  The Lean statements are \code{consts\_classified} and the three companions
  \code{constOrigin\_not\_ctorOf}, \code{constOrigin\_not\_indInfo} and
  \code{constOrigin\_of\_constants}. The well-formedness hypothesis of the first is unused --- it
  is named with a leading underscore --- and call sites supply it for nothing.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{asm:UpstreamAsks-constsOrigin}
  Each is a one-line projection of the classification conjunct of the upstream ask bundle. The
  exclusion and uniqueness directions are facts about lean4lean's environment well-formedness that
  the pinned lean4lean does not export, so they are assumed once and unpacked here, and every
  consumer then takes one identical, auditable premise that a pin bump discharges at a stroke with
  no statement changing shape.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The coordinates of a name are unique]
  \label{lem:IndInfo-inj}
  \uses{def:UpstreamAsks, def:IndInfo, def:IndArity, def:CtorOf, def:IndDeclOf, def:CasesOnShape}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.IndInfo.inj, LeanToLambdaBox.CtorOf.inj, LeanToLambdaBox.CasesOnShape.inj, LeanToLambdaBox.IndArity.inj, LeanToLambdaBox.IndInfo.indDeclOf, LeanToLambdaBox.indBlock_uniq, LeanToLambdaBox.indBlockBelow_type_uniq, LeanToLambdaBox.addConst_foldlM_name_inj}
  \leanok
  The \lbox{} coordinates the eraser emits are functions of the source name. The block declaring a
  given type former is unique, so its block kername, its index, its parameter count and its
  per-constructor field counts are determined by the name; a constructor belongs to one inductive
  at one index; an eliminator's segmentation is unique; a type former declared below the
  environment is declared by the environment's own list; two blocks declaring the same former are
  the same block; and a block declares each of its formers under exactly one name.

  The Lean statements are the four injectivity lemmas, the declaration lemma
  \code{IndInfo.indDeclOf}, the two block-uniqueness lemmas, and the name-injectivity of a
  successful environment-extension fold. All eight take the upstream ask bundle. The eliminator
  injectivity \code{CasesOnShape.inj} has exactly one occurrence in the repository --- its own
  definition. Together these are what make two independently exhibited witnesses --- an
  eliminator's block and an arity's block, say --- identifiable, and hence what make two erasure
  derivations of the same program agree on constructor blocks.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{asm:UpstreamAsks-constsOrigin}
  The injectivity statements are projections of the ask. The two block lemmas are the workhorses:
  a successful environment-extension fold has injective names, so two blocks below the environment
  that both declare a former must coincide, and a block declares each former under one name.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A value of a relevant inductive type is not erasable]
  \label{thm:not_erasable_of_informative}
  \uses{def:Erasable, def:InformativeInd, def:IndDeclOf, def:UpstreamAsks, def:IndBlockBelow, def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.not_erasable_of_informative, LeanToLambdaBox.indSpine_not_prop, LeanToLambdaBox.peel_ne_prop, LeanToLambdaBox.indSpine_ne_forallE}
  \leanok
  If a type former is inductively declared and its declared result sort never evaluates to zero
  --- an \emph{informative} inductive --- then no term typed at a spine headed by it is erasable:
  it is neither a proof nor a type former, so the box rule cannot reach it.

  The Lean statement \code{not\_erasable\_of\_informative} takes the environment's
  well-formedness, the upstream ask bundle, and a context of types; its two supporting lemmas
  \code{indSpine\_not\_prop} and \code{peel\_ne\_prop} carry the propositional half, and
  \code{indSpine\_ne\_forallE} the product half.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:IndInfo-inj, asm:UpstreamAsks-constsOrigin, asm:UpstreamAsks-mkAppsInv, asm:UpstreamAsks-constArityInv, asm:lean4lean-trust}
  \Erasable{} offers two disjuncts. The proof disjunct would make the inductive spine a
  proposition: the spine's typing is peeled onto the former's declared type, whose result sort is
  never zero, and peeling such a type can never end at \code{Sort 0}, since each peel step keeps
  the two types definitionally equal and sort inversion would then equate a never-zero sort with
  \code{Sort 0}. The type-former disjunct would make the spine definitionally equal to a sort or a
  product, which the declaration data refutes.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  This is the non-erasability side condition of the whole development: it is what makes a
  first-order answer survive erasure un-boxed, and the first-order layer of
  Chapter~\ref{chap:correctness} consumes it. Its measured footprint is the standard one together
  with \code{sorryAx}. [S, Sec. 7.3]'s erasure relation excludes the box rule by exactly such an
  irrelevance side condition; this is its model-side negation.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Peeling the typing of a translated spine]
  \label{lem:trExprS_spine_peel}
  \uses{def:IndBlockBelow, def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.trExprS_spine_peel}
  \leanok
  If a head and a whole application both translate, and the head is typed, then there is a list of
  translated arguments of the right length and the application's type is reached by peeling the
  head's type through them.

  The Lean statement \code{trExprS\_spine\_peel} quantifies over the argument list and returns the
  argument translations index-wise, together with the peeled type and the application's typing. It
  takes no upstream ask --- contrast Theorem~\ref{thm:not_erasable_of_informative}, whose
  \emph{untranslated} spine does --- and it is the peeling engine of the two saturation results
  below.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{asm:lean4lean-trust}
  Induction along the spine. The application arm of lean4lean's translation relation already
  carries the typing of the function at a product and of the argument at its domain, so no
  upstream ask is needed; unique typing collapses the two typings of the head.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The discriminant of a saturated eliminator has its own inductive type]
  \label{thm:elim_major}
  \uses{def:CasesOnShape, def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.elim_major, LeanToLambdaBox.peel_major}
  \leanok
  In a closed, well-typed application of an inductive's eliminator with the segmentation the
  eliminator's own block fixes --- so many arguments before the discriminant, one minor premise
  per constructor after it, and possibly extra arguments --- the translation of the discriminant
  is typed at a spine headed by that inductive.

  The Lean statement \code{elim\_major} is at the empty modelled local context, takes the
  eliminator's shape predicate and the two length equations, and is the one kernel corollary of
  its file that takes \emph{no} upstream ask.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:trExprS_spine_peel, asm:lean4lean-trust}
  The eliminator's shape predicate places the major premise at a known position of the declared
  type; Lemma~\ref{lem:trExprS_spine_peel} peels the spine's typing onto that type, and
  \code{peel\_major} reads the position off, typing that argument at the inductive's spine.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  This is the typing premise of the iota arm of Chapter~\ref{chap:correctness}'s simulation: it is
  what lets the simulation conclude that the scrutinee evaluates to a constructor of the right
  inductive, hence that the \lbox{} case node selects the matching branch. It is [S, Sec. 7.4]'s
  case step.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[A product telescope over an inductive spine]
  \label{def:PiSpine}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.PiSpine}
  \leanok
  \code{PiSpine} says that a type is a telescope of exactly a given number of products whose body
  is an application headed by a given inductive --- the shape a constructor's declared type has.

  The Lean definition \code{PiSpine} is by recursion on the number of products.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Peeling a constructor's type exhausts its telescope]
  \label{lem:peel_piSpine}
  \uses{def:PiSpine, def:IndBlockBelow, def:UpstreamAsks, def:IndDeclOf, def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.peel_piSpine, LeanToLambdaBox.peel_piSpine_head}
  \leanok
  Peeling a type definitionally equal to a product telescope over an inductive spine, at arguments
  whose result is again an inductive spine, consumes the telescope exactly; and peeling it to its
  end reaches the telescope's own result spine.

  The Lean statements \code{peel\_piSpine} and \code{peel\_piSpine\_head} take the declaredness of
  both inductives involved, as well as the upstream ask bundle.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:not_erasable_of_informative, asm:UpstreamAsks-constArityInv, asm:lean4lean-trust}
  A short spine would end at a product where an inductive spine is wanted; a long one would
  require an inductive spine to be a product. Both are refuted by the statement that an inductive
  spine is definitionally equal to no product type, which is the upstream ask read off the
  declaration data and is proved alongside
  Theorem~\ref{thm:not_erasable_of_informative}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A well-typed constructor spine is saturated]
  \label{thm:ctor_saturated}
  \uses{def:CtorOf, def:IndInfo, def:IndArity, def:UpstreamAsks, def:PiSpine, def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.ctor_saturated, LeanToLambdaBox.CtorOf.ctorResult_at}
  \leanok
  A closed application of the $k$-th constructor of an inductive whose translation is typed at a
  spine of that inductive has exactly as many arguments as the block has parameters plus that
  constructor has fields.

  The Lean statement \code{ctor\_saturated} is at the empty modelled local context and takes the
  constructor's coordinates and the block's arity data explicitly; its helper
  \code{CtorOf.ctorResult\_at} exhibits the constructor's declared type as such a telescope.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:peel_piSpine, lem:trExprS_spine_peel, lem:IndInfo-inj, asm:UpstreamAsks-constsOrigin, asm:lean4lean-trust}
  The constructor's declared type is a product telescope of exactly that length over the
  inductive's spine: \code{CtorOf.ctorResult\_at} identifies the constructor's block with the
  arity's block by uniqueness and reads the registered constant off the block's staging.
  Lemma~\ref{lem:trExprS_spine_peel} peels the translated spine's typing onto it, and
  Lemma~\ref{lem:peel_piSpine} forces the length.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  This is the fact that would license \lbox{}'s \emph{block} form for constructors --- the
  Lean-side counterpart of MetaRocq's \code{constructors\_as\_blocks} --- even though this eraser
  emits applied form anyway (Definition~\ref{def:Erasure-visitExpr}). A reader who knows [S] will
  expect the block form and must be told the frontend chose otherwise.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[The eliminator's block is the inductive's block]
  \label{thm:CasesOnShape-agree}
  \uses{def:CasesOnShape, def:UpstreamAsks, def:IndArity, def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.CasesOnShape.agree, LeanToLambdaBox.CasesOnShape.indBlockBelow, LeanToLambdaBox.IndArity.indBlockBelow}
  \leanok
  If a constant is an inductive's eliminator at a given segmentation and the inductive has
  source-side arity data, then the number of arguments before the discriminant decomposes as the
  parameters, one motive and the remaining indices, and the number of minor premises equals the
  number of constructors.

  The Lean statement \code{CasesOnShape.agree} takes the upstream ask bundle; its two helpers
  exhibit, from each of the two hypotheses, a well-formed declaration list below the environment.
  This is what lets the source-side iota rule, which counts \emph{parameters}, and the \lbox{}
  case node, which counts \emph{fields}, be about the same block --- the pivot of
  Chapter~\ref{chap:correctness}'s iota arm. Its measured footprint contains no \code{sorryAx}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:IndInfo-inj, asm:UpstreamAsks-constsOrigin}
  Each of the two hypotheses exhibits a well-formed declaration list below the environment; block
  uniqueness identifies the two blocks and type-name uniqueness identifies the former inside it,
  after which the arithmetic is read off the same block twice.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A tabled constant is a constant of the model]
  \label{lem:constants_of_tabled}
  \uses{def:ErasureSpec, def:Witness-SourceTableAdequate, def:TableSafe, def:lean4lean-grounding}
  \lean{LeanToLambdaBox.constants_of_tabled}
  \leanok
  A constant for which the reified compiler table supplies a body is a declared constant of the
  model.

  The Lean statement \code{constants\_of\_tabled} takes the specification bundle, the table's
  adequacy and the table's safety hypothesis, and concludes only the existence of the model
  constant, not its shape.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{asm:ErasureSpec-decl_adequate}
  Table adequacy pins the constant in the elaboration environment, the table's safety hypothesis
  gives its safety level, and the declaration-adequacy field translates it into the model.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  This is one of the two halves of the exclusion the environment relation of
  Chapter~\ref{chap:lowering} needs at a tabled constant; the other half is
  \code{constOrigin\_of\_constants} (Lemma~\ref{lem:consts_classified}). Their join --- that a
  tabled constant is neither a constructor nor an inductive type name in the model --- is an
  upstream ask and is \emph{not} proved here; until the pin bump lands it stands as a premise.
\end{remark}

\section{Deviations from the paper, and caveats}
\label{sec:eraser:caveats}

The eraser departs from [S] and [L] in five places that matter for reading the rest of this
blueprint. \emph{Applied constructors} (F-ETA): MetaRocq's erasure target and agda2lambox both
emit constructors as blocks carrying their arguments, while this eraser emits a bare constructor
node applied through \lbox{} application, which is why every semantic statement of
Chapter~\ref{chap:lambdabox} is pinned with the block-constructor flag off and why
Theorem~\ref{thm:ctor_saturated}, which would license the block form, is proved and not used for
it. \emph{Bare fixpoints} (F-ETA): the emitted fixpoint bodies are unapplied, so MetaRocq's
expanded-program predicate is false on every benchmark program, and the capstone concludes the
peregrine well-formedness predicate instead. \emph{A preparation pass with no MetaCoq
counterpart}: \code{@[macro\_inline]} unfolding, matcher inlining, \code{\_unsafe\_rec}
redirection and \code{@[csimp]} rewriting are what Lean's compiler needs and Rocq has no analogue
of; the pass's doc comment says it ``may make the expression ill-typed'', and
\code{ConfigPinned} pins the \code{@[csimp]} walk off. \emph{The oracle reroute}: MetaRocq's
erasability test runs on the kernel it is verified against by construction, whereas here the test
had to be re-implemented on lean4lean's verified executable checker and proved sound
(Theorem~\ref{thm:isErasable-WF}), with the gap between the checker's obligation and an actual
run closed separately (Theorem~\ref{thm:Oracle-kernel_isErasable_sound}), at the price of a
33-name measured axiom footprint including \code{sorryAx}. \emph{Three configuration layers
declared out of scope}: machine \code{Nat}, \code{@[csimp]} and \code{@[extern]}-as-axiom are
exactly the features [R] describes as implemented, and exactly the three fields
\code{ConfigPinned} pins away from their defaults.

Four caveats travel with every statement of this chapter. Two \code{EraserAsks} fields are
documented refuted in general, so results taking the bundle are conditionally vacuous on an
environment exhibiting a counterexample. A \code{panic!} or \code{unreachable!} inside the eraser
does not abort the run but yields $\Box$ and exits successfully, so several findings --- F-SPARSE
above all --- manifest as a wrong \code{.ast} rather than as a failure. The theorem stack stops at
the \code{Program} value: the S-expression printer that turns it into bytes is an unverified
\code{partial def} that nothing in the development mentions. And the relational pass interface
\code{LBPassR} declared at the end of \code{ErasureSpec.lean}, in MetaRocq's
\code{optimize\_correct} shape, has no consumer anywhere in the repository and its doc comment
forward-references a theorem that does not exist; it is an aspiration, not a current interface.
