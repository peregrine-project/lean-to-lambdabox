\section{The composite relation}
\label{sec:refinement:composite}

$\Erases$ relates \code{Lean.Expr} to \lbox{}; $\Lower$ relates \lbox{} to \lbox{}, adding Lean's
compilation steps. Neither alone is what the eraser emits, so \code{ErasesLB} composes them; the rest of
the chapter proves \textbf{T8}, that \code{Erasure.visitExpr} refines the composite.

\begin{definition}[The composite ErasesLB]
\label{def:ErasesLB}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLB}
\leanok
\uses{def:Erases, def:Lower}
\lead{In short} $e$ is \hl{composite-related} to $t$ when some $t_0$ both erases $e$ and lowers to $t$:
\[ \mathrm{ErasesLB}\ \Sigma\ Us\ \Gamma\ \Delta\ e\ t \;:=\; \exists t_0,\; \Erases\ \Sigma\ Us\ \Delta\ e\ t_0 \wedge \Lower\ \Gamma\ t_0\ t . \]
\begin{itemize}
\item erase first, lower second; disjoint parameters ($\Erases$: model, scope, context; $\Lower$: the
\lbox{} environment only);
\item $\Erases$ alone will not do: the eraser emits \code{.case}/\code{.fix} nodes no \code{Lean.Expr}
constructor matches.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The composite at a case alternative]
\label{def:ErasesLBAlt}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLBAlt, LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLBAlts}
\leanok
\uses{def:Erases, def:Lower}
\lead{In short} A minor premise $m$ is composite-related to an alternative $(\mathit{names},
\mathit{body})$ at field arity $\mathit{nf}$ when $m$ erases to a term whose lambda-chain \code{LowerAlt}
peels into it.
\begin{itemize}
\item binder names are free; only the count (what \code{iota\_red} reads) is pinned;
\item \code{ErasesLBAlts}: the pointwise lift, carrying $|\mathit{minors}| = |\mathit{nfs}| =
|\mathit{alts}|$.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Alternatives inside a block]
\label{def:ErasesLBFixAlt}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLBFixAlt, LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLBFixAlts}
\leanok
\uses{def:ErasesLBAlt, def:ConstToFVar}
\lead{In short} One alternative \emph{inside} a mutual block: Definition~\ref{def:ErasesLBAlt} with the
block's kernames rewritten to fix variables by \code{ConstToFVar}, binder \emph{count} pinned.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
\lead{Caveat} The in-block term composite \code{ErasesLBFix} (three existentials: $\Erases$, $\Lower$,
\code{ConstToFVar}) is Definition~\ref{def:ErasesLBFix} of Chapter~\ref{chap:lowering}, not redefined
here.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[A spine premise, folded]
\label{lem:erasesLB_of_spine}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.erasesLB_of_spine}
\leanok
\uses{def:ErasesLB, def:Erases, def:Lower}
\lead{In short} For equal-length lists, ``each argument erases to some $a_0$ the pass lowers to the
emitted argument'' \hl{is} the composite premise, as an \code{Iff} plus an unused length equation ---
the one \code{Lower.mkApps} consumes at its call site.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{def:ErasesLB}
\lead{Idea} Unfolding \code{ErasesLB} turns one side into the other syntactically: \code{Iff.rfl}.
\end{proof}

\section{Introduction rules of the composite}
\label{sec:refinement:intro}

These eight rules are the composite's introduction form: \code{box}, \code{app}, \code{lit}, \code{proj},
\code{ctor} match [JACM, Fig.~18]; \code{fix} and \code{cases} exist only because Lean's kernel has neither
\code{tFix} nor \code{tCase}. This section measures \stChecked{} clean, no \code{sorryAx}.

\begin{lemma}[Erasure is a spine congruence]
\label{lem:Erases-mkApps}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.Erases.mkApps, LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLB.exists_mid}
\leanok
\uses{def:Erases, def:ErasesLB, def:Lower, def:LBTerm-mkApps}
\lead{In short} A spine erases \hl{pointwise}, and a spine of composites splits into a shared middle
list.
\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Given} (i) the head and each argument erase, target argument lists of equal length; \lead{Then}
the whole spine erases to the corresponding \lbox{} spine;
\item \lead{Given} (ii) index-wise \code{ErasesLB} on an argument list; \lead{Then} a middle list of the
same length with $\Erases\ \mathit{args}[i]\ \mathit{mid}[i]$ and $\Lower\ \Gamma\ \mathit{mid}[i]\
\mathit{args}'[i]$ at every index.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{def:Erases}
\lead{Idea} (i) induction on the argument list, applying $\Erases$'s app rule per step. (ii) collects the
per-argument existentials via the choice-free helper \code{exists\_list\_of\_index}. Both power the
\code{ctor}/\code{cases} spine rules below.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The box rule]
\label{lem:ErasesLB-box}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLB.box}
\leanok
\uses{def:ErasesLB, def:Erasable, def:Erases, def:lean4lean-grounding}
\lead{In short} An \Erasable{} source term is composite-related to \hl{$\Box$}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Given} $e$ has a lean4lean translation $ve$ in $\Delta$; $ve$ is \Erasable{} (a proof or a
type former);
\item \lead{Then} $\mathrm{ErasesLB}\ \Sigma\ Us\ \Gamma\ \Delta\ e\ \Box$, any $\Gamma$.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{def:Erases, def:Lower}
\lead{Idea} Intermediate $\Box$; source factor is $\Erases$'s box rule (Letouzey's [L]); target factor
is the pass's box rule, counterpart $\Box\,x\to\Box$. Premises live over lean4lean's \code{VEnv}, touching
only a \emph{definition}, so the footprint stays clean.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Application]
\label{lem:ErasesLB-app}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLB.app}
\leanok
\uses{def:ErasesLB}
\lead{In short} The composite is a \hl{congruence} in both factors of an application.
\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Given} $\mathrm{ErasesLB}\ f\ f'$ and $\mathrm{ErasesLB}\ a\ a'$;
\item \lead{Then} $\mathrm{ErasesLB}\ (f\,a)\ (f'\,a')$.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{def:Erases, def:Lower}
\lead{Idea} Destructure both composites into their intermediates and pair them.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Constructors, in applied form]
\label{lem:ErasesLB-ctor}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLB.ctor_head, LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLB.ctor}
\leanok
\uses{def:ErasesLB, def:CtorOf, def:IndInfo}
\lead{In short} A bare constructor becomes the \hl{empty} node; a spine becomes \code{mkApps} of it, at
any arity.
\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Given} a constructor at index $k$ of a block with model data \code{IndInfo env I iid np nfs};
\item \lead{Then} \code{ctor\_head}: composite-related to \code{.construct iid k []};
\item \lead{Given} argument lists of equal length, index-wise composite-related; \lead{Then}
\code{ctor}: composite-related to \code{LBTerm.mkApps (.construct iid k []) args'}, universe levels
dropped.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{lem:Erases-mkApps, def:Erases, def:Lower}
\lead{Idea} \code{ctor\_head}: $\Erases$'s \code{ctor} rule paired with the pass's empty-arity
\code{construct} congruence. \code{ctor}: split the composite spine, rebuild the source side
(\code{Erases.mkApps}) and target side (\code{Lower.mkApps} over \code{construct}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\lead{Deviation} Pins Lean's \textbf{applied} constructor convention (F-ETA2); \code{agda2lambox}
eta-expands to fully applied blocks instead.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Literals]
\label{lem:ErasesLB-lit}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLB.lit}
\leanok
\uses{def:ErasesLB, def:Erases}
\lead{In short} A literal is composite-related to whatever its \hl{one-step kernel unfolding} is.
\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Given} the model declares the literal's constructors (\code{env.ContainsLits l});
\item \lead{Then} composite-related to the image of \code{l.toConstructor}.
\end{itemize}
\lead{Lean} at pinned \code{nat = .peano}, the eraser rebuilds a \code{Nat} literal as its peano tower
(step 2, Lemma~\ref{lem:step_visitLiteral}).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{def:Erases}
\lead{Idea} The pass is the identity here, so the composite inherits $\Erases$'s \code{lit} rule.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Projections]
\label{lem:ErasesLB-proj}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLB.proj}
\leanok
\uses{def:ErasesLB, def:IndInfo, def:InformativeInd}
\lead{In short} A structure projection becomes the \lbox{} \code{.proj} node when the block is
\hl{informative}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Given} \code{.proj S i e}; $S$ a single-constructor block with $np$ parameters, $nf$ fields;
$S$ informative (not \code{Prop}); the field index in range; the discriminee composite-related to $t$;
\item \lead{Then} composite-related to \code{.proj} $\langle iid, np, i\rangle\ t$.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{def:Erases, def:Lower}
\lead{Idea} One \code{proj} rule of $\Erases$ over the discriminee's erasure, then the pass's \code{proj}
congruence. Informativity matters: without it the emitted node is stuck on every value.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A block member's constant becomes a fix node]
\label{lem:ErasesLB-fix}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLB.fix}
\leanok
\uses{def:ErasesLB, def:LowerBlock, def:ElimDecl, def:ConstOrigin, def:Kername}
\lead{In short} A reference to a block member's own constant becomes the block's \hl{\code{.fix}} node.
\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Given} \code{LowerBlock} holds of the block's data; \code{cn} is the block's $j$-th member, a
model definition, opaque or axiom; \code{toKername cn} is \textbf{not} a runtime key of $\Gamma$;
\item \lead{Then} \code{.const cn us} is composite-related to \code{.fix defs j}.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{def:Erases, def:Lower, lem:Lower-fixConst-prime}
\lead{Idea} $\Erases$'s \code{const} rule sends the constant to its kername; the pass's
\code{Lower.fixConst'} turns that into \code{.fix defs j}. The negative premise is the pass's own guard: a
block member is never a pruned eliminator key.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\lead{Why} Lean's kernel has no \code{tFix}: top-level recursion is compiled, so this is a \emph{pass}
rule, not an erasure rule. \code{toKername} is not injective (Lemma~\ref{lem:toKername_not_injective}).
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[A saturated eliminator spine becomes a case node]
\label{lem:ErasesLB-cases}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLB.cases}
\leanok
\uses{def:ErasesLB, def:ErasesLBAlt, def:ElimDecl, def:ConstOrigin, def:ProjInfo, def:CtorOf,
def:IndInfo, def:lean4lean-grounding}
\lead{In short} A saturated \code{casesOn} spine becomes the \lbox{} \hl{\code{.case}} node.
\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Given} \code{con} declared as an eliminator for block $iid$ with $np$ parameters, $dp$
dropped arguments (parameters, motive, indices) and per-constructor field arities $nfs$; a plain model
constant; the spine has exactly $dp + 1 + |nfs|$ arguments;
\item argument $dp$ composite-related to the discriminee, the $|nfs|$ minors after it to the alternatives
at their field arities; every dropped argument translated and satisfying \code{ProjInfo}; a
\textbf{global} classification of every model constant as constructor, inductive or plain;
model/context well-formedness;
\item \lead{Then} the source spine is composite-related to \code{.case} $(iid, np)\ \mathit{disc}\
\mathit{alts}$.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{lem:Erases-mkApps, def:Erases, def:Lower, thm:Erases-exists_of_trExprS_of_projInfo,
lem:Lower-fixConst-prime}
\lead{Idea} Split the spine into the first $dp$ arguments, the discriminee, and the minors. $\Erases$ is
a congruence over the \emph{whole} spine, so the dropped arguments still need an erasure image, supplied
from the translation witness and the global classification by \code{exists\_of\_trExprS\_of\_projInfo}
and collected with the minors' images. \code{Erases.mkApps} erases the whole spine to a \code{.const}
head applied to the concatenation, which \code{Lower.elimApp'} rewrites to the \code{.case} node.
\end{proof}

\section{The same rules inside a mutual block}
\label{sec:refinement:block}

Inside a mutual block the eraser emits a \textbf{free variable} where the source names a sibling by
\code{.const} --- a pair neither $\Erases$ nor $\Lower$ relates. \code{ErasesLBFix} carries a third
factor, \code{ConstToFVar}, and every rule above has a twin.

\begin{lemma}[Block rewriting over a spine]
\label{lem:ConstToFVar-mkApps}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.ConstToFVar.mkApps}
\leanok
\uses{def:ConstToFVar, def:LBTerm-mkApps}
\lead{In short} \code{ConstToFVar} \hl{lifts to spines}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Given} it relates two heads, and two equal-length argument lists index-wise;
\item \lead{Then} it relates the two spines.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{def:ConstToFVar}
\lead{Idea} Induction on the argument list, one \code{app} congruence per step.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Factoring the in-block composite]
\label{lem:ErasesLBFix-of_erasesLB}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLBFix.of_erasesLB, LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLBFix.exists_erasesLB,
LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLBFix.exists_mid}
\leanok
\uses{def:ErasesLB, def:ConstToFVar, def:ErasesLBFix}
\lead{In short} The in-block composite \hl{splits} into the ambient composite followed by block
rewriting, and recomposes from them.
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{of\_erasesLB}: post-composes;
\item \code{exists\_erasesLB}: the converse reading;
\item \code{exists\_mid}: the spine version, producing one middle list of the right length.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{def:ErasesLB}
\lead{Idea} Both directions re-associate the three existentials against the two of \code{ErasesLB}; the
spine version collects the per-index pairs with \code{exists\_list\_of\_index}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The block branch's own rule]
\label{lem:ErasesLBFix-fixvar}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLBFix.fixvar}
\leanok
\uses{def:ErasesLBFix, def:ConstOrigin, def:ElimDecl, def:Kername}
\lead{In short} A reference to the block's \hl{own} name becomes that member's fix variable.
\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Given} the block's $j$-th kername is \code{toKername cn}; the $j$-th identifier is $x$;
\code{toKername cn} is not a runtime key of $\Gamma$; \code{cn} a plain model constant;
\item \lead{Then} \code{.const cn us} is in-block related to \code{.fvar x}.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{def:Erases, def:Lower, def:ConstToFVar}
\lead{Idea} A three-factor term: $\Erases$'s \code{const} rule (constant to kername); $\Lower$'s
\code{const} rule (a non-eliminator kername survives); \code{ConstToFVar}'s \code{hit} rule (the block's
$j$-th kername to its $j$-th identifier) --- the one step with no ambient \code{ErasesLB} counterpart.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The eight rules, replayed inside a block]
\label{lem:ErasesLBFix-box}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLBFix.box, LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLBFix.app,
LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLBFix.ctor_head, LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLBFix.ctor,
LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLBFix.lit, LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLBFix.proj, LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLBFix.cases,
LeanToLambdaBox.ErasesLBFix.fix}
\leanok
\uses{def:ErasesLBFix, def:ErasesLBFixAlt}
\lead{In short} Each of the eight rules of \S\ref{sec:refinement:intro} holds \hl{inside a block} too.
\begin{itemize}
\item same premises plus the block indices;
\item the \code{cases} twin takes \code{ErasesLBFixAlts} for its minors;
\item the \code{fix} twin is about a \emph{different}, inner block, whose \code{.fix} node the outer
block's rewriting leaves alone.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{lem:ErasesLBFix-of_erasesLB, lem:ConstToFVar-mkApps, lem:ErasesLB-box, lem:ErasesLB-app,
lem:ErasesLB-ctor, lem:ErasesLB-lit, lem:ErasesLB-proj, lem:ErasesLB-cases, lem:ErasesLB-fix}
\lead{Idea} Split the in-block composite into \code{ErasesLB} plus \code{ConstToFVar}, apply the ambient
lemma, recompose with the matching congruence. All eight measured \code{sorryAx}-free.
\end{proof}

\section{The iota reversal bridge}
\label{sec:refinement:iota}

\lbox{}'s iota rule (MetaRocq's \code{iota\_red}) is one simultaneous, \textbf{reversed} substitution of
fields into the alternative's body. The eraser instead applies a lambda-telescope to the fields
\emph{in order}, via a \textbf{chain of beta steps}; the two agree only over closed field values. The
module mentions neither $\Erases$ nor lean4lean, so it is \code{sorryAx}-free.

\begin{lemma}[Head congruence for an application spine]
\label{lem:wcbvEval_mkApps_head_congr}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.wcbvEval_mkApps_head_congr}
\leanok
\uses{def:WcbvEval, def:LBTerm-mkApps}
\lead{In short} A one-directional simulation of heads \hl{lifts to spines}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Given} every evaluation of $g$ is also an evaluation of $h$;
\item \lead{Then} every evaluation of $g\,a_1\ldots a_n$ is an evaluation of $h\,a_1\ldots a_n$.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{def:WcbvEval}
\lead{Idea} Induction down the spine: every \code{.app} rule of $\WcbvEval$ dispatches on the function's
\emph{value} first, so the head may be replaced rule by rule --- reconciling the beta chain's
innermost-first order with $\WcbvEval$'s outermost-first dispatch.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fields of a constructor value are values]
\label{lem:value_mkApps_construct_args}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.value_mkApps_construct_args}
\leanok
\uses{def:Value, def:LBTerm-mkApps}
\lead{In short} A constructor value's \hl{fields are themselves values}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Given} \code{mkApps (.construct iid c []) args} is a value;
\item \lead{Then} so is every element of \code{args}.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{def:Value}
\lead{Idea} Strong recursion on the spine length, inverting the constructor-application arm one argument
at a time. Supplies the bridge's ``every field evaluates to itself'' premise at its call site.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A beta-chain of field applications is iota\_red]
\label{prop:wcbvEval_mkApps_mkLambdas_substList}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.wcbvEval_mkApps_mkLambdas_substList}
\leanok
\uses{def:WcbvEval, def:mkLambdas, def:LBClosed, def:LBTerm-subst, def:LBTerm-mkApps}
\lead{In short} A beta chain over an alternative's telescope \hl{agrees with \code{iota\_red}}, over
closed field values.
\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Given} the binder-name list has exactly the fields' length; every field is a closed value
(evaluates to itself, no loose de Bruijn index);
\item \lead{Then} every evaluation of \code{mkApps (mkLambdas names body) fields} is an evaluation of
\code{LBTerm.substList fields.reverse body}.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{lem:wcbvEval_mkApps_head_congr, def:LBTerm-subst}
\lead{Idea} Induction on the fields: each beta contraction is a head replacement fed to
Lemma~\ref{lem:wcbvEval_mkApps_head_congr}; the non-beta \code{.app} rules are refuted by determinism.
The de Bruijn arithmetic is the iterated-substitution lemma plus the reversal lemma of \code{Closed.lean}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\lead{Why} Closedness is faithfulness, not convenience: at two fields the beta chain reduces to
$\mathrm{subst}\ f_1\ 0\ (\mathrm{subst}\ f_0\ 1\ \mathit{body})$ while \code{substList} gives
$\mathrm{subst}\ f_0\ 0\ (\mathrm{subst}\ f_1\ 0\ \mathit{body})$; the two agree only when
$\mathrm{subst}\ f_0\ 0\ f_1 = f_1$, which fails for a value with a loose index (e.g. $f_1$ a lambda over
variable $1$). MetaRocq's \code{closedn 0} convention is \emph{needed}. Argued in the module's docstring,
not mechanised.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[The bridge fires at two fields]
\label{lem:wcbvEval_mkApps_mkLambdas_substList_fires}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.wcbvEval_mkApps_mkLambdas_substList_fires}
\leanok
\uses{def:WcbvEval, def:eraseFlags}
\lead{In short} A \hl{closed, hypothesis-free instance}: the two-variable telescope over the inner
variable, applied to $\Box$ and a lambda returning $\Box$, evaluates to $\Box$.
\lead{Non-vacuity} two fields is the first regime the flat slice could not reach.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{prop:wcbvEval_mkApps_mkLambdas_substList}
\lead{Idea} Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:wcbvEval_mkApps_mkLambdas_substList} at the two explicit fields,
discharging value and closedness by the box and lambda rules and by computation, and supplying the beta
chain directly.
\end{proof}

\section{The induction's interfaces: motives, bodies, steps}
\label{sec:refinement:motives}

The eraser is \emph{impure} (reads the environment, mints fresh \code{FVarId}s), \emph{partial}
(\code{visitExpr} and seventeen siblings form one \code{mutual} block closed by \code{partial\_fixpoint},
with \code{panic!} fall-throughs), and \emph{discovers} the environment while walking the term --- so the
proof is a fixpoint induction with one motive per member, each \emph{admissible} and preserved by the
functional's \emph{step}. \code{Motives.lean} declares the eighteen abstract bodies as top-level
definitions, so a step lemma is a theorem about a named function, proved in a file with no induction.

\begin{definition}[What a successful sub-run concludes]
\label{def:RunRefines}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.RunRefines, LeanToLambdaBox.RunRefinesAlt, LeanToLambdaBox.HeadRefines}
\leanok
\uses{def:ErasesLBMode, def:SpecEnv, def:Erasure-RunConcl, def:IndRegistryModelled}
\lead{In short} \code{RunRefines} bundles what a successful eraser run \hl{establishes about its final
state}.
\begin{itemize}

\begin{lemma}[The pair the eraser installs is a valid description]
\label{lem:blockKeyed_install}
\lean{LeanToLambdaBox.blockKeyed_install, LeanToLambdaBox.env_motive_tabled}
\leanok
\uses{def:BlockKeyed, def:TableBlocks, def:UpstreamAsks, def:Erasure-visitMutual,
def:fixvarMap}
\lead{In short} The pair \code{visitMutual} installs for a block \hl{satisfies \code{BlockKeyed}}.
\begin{itemize}
\item (i) the map equation is the block branch's reader extension; the length condition is the identifier
loop's; distinctness is a supplied hypothesis, the F-UNSAFEREC guard read back off a successful block
exit (\code{run\_rec\_exit\_reg}'s fifth conclusion); key separation is the table's own;
\item (ii) a constant with a compiler-table body is a plain model constant, given it is neither a
constructor nor a type former.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{asm:UpstreamAsks-constsOrigin}
\lead{Idea} Four conjuncts, one per clause of \code{BlockKeyed}: the first three read off their own
source, the fourth off the supplied \code{Nodup} hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\lead{Caveat} \textbf{Both results are unconsumed}, deliberately: motive 6 reports only registration, and
the block's content is read off the final state by \code{SpecEnv} instead. Distinctness of a block's
keys is not an assumed field: \code{Erasure.visitMutual} refuses a block whose members collide
under \code{toKername} (F-UNSAFEREC), and \code{ErasureRun.run\_rec\_exit\_nodup} reads that guard back
as a fact about a successful run.
\end{remark}

\section*{Deviations from the paper; caveats}

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Deviation} Applied-form constructors (F-ETA2): the node carries no fields, arguments ride the
spine, per the pinned \code{eraseFlags}; \code{agda2lambox} eta-expands to blocks instead.
\item \lead{Deviation} \code{fix} and \code{cases} are compilation steps, not erasure rules: Lean's kernel
has neither \code{tFix} nor \code{tCase}, so $\Erases$ has eleven rules and none of its own for \code{fix}
or \code{cases}; \code{ErasesLB} exists to put them back.
\item \lead{Deviation} The box rule is decided by an external oracle, not derived from typing: step 1
spends its soundness and is the chapter's only \code{sorryAx} entry point.
\item \lead{Deviation} The iota rule's closedness side condition is faithfulness, not slack: the reversal
bridge is false without it (\S\ref{sec:refinement:iota}'s two-field counterexample).
\item \lead{Deviation} The pinned configuration is not the default: \code{ConfigPinned} differs from
\code{\#erase}'s default on three of five fields, so the machine-\code{Nat}, \code{@[csimp]} and
\code{@[extern]} arms sit outside the statement's scope, not outside the code.
\item \lead{Caveat} T8 concerns a term, never an environment; that half stays an assumption at every
rung. The two T8 theorems' clean footprints are an artefact of hypothesised steps. \lead{Deviation}
The single fixed level scope of the eighteen motives and the universally quantified
$\Gamma_{\mathrm{spec}}$ of the run predicate are chosen divergences from [JACM], not forced ones: both
are \stOpen{}, specified as units U5--U6 of \code{doc/rework/09-REPAIRS-W7.md}.
\end{itemize}
