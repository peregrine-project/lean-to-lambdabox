\chapter{Scope and the fragment}\label{chap:scope}

\section*{At a glance}
\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Goal} State which \code{\#erase} inputs the verification covers, and why: every closed,
  typed program that Metalean can express and that the eraser handles without a known defect.
\item \lead{Main results} The fragment in two phases: Phase I adds to the $\lambda\Pi$ fragment
  everything about inductive types that the eraser handles; Phase II adds recursors and quotients.
  The divergence entries DV-23 to DV-26 and the choices of Section~\ref{sec:scope-decisions} fix
  its edges.
\item \lead{Status} \stShipping{} the fragment test of Phase I
  (Definition~\ref{def:Erasure-collectDeps}), which the hypothesis \code{hfrag} of the final
  theorem names; \stPlanned{} its lower bound \code{collectDeps\_ok\_of}
  (Chapter~\ref{chap:core}). The corpus counts below are those of the shipping test.
\item \lead{Reference} MetaRocq \code{erase\_correct}'s hypotheses \code{wf\_ext},
  \code{Sigma} $\sim$\code{\_ext X}, \code{welltyped}.
\end{itemize}

\section{The principle}\label{sec:scope-principle}

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Expressible in Metalean} the program's declarations are those Metalean's loader
  accepts (\leanfile{Metalean/Checker/Slow/Loader.lean}) and its terms those Metalean's translator
  translates (\leanfile{Metalean/Frontend/Translate.lean}). The typing hypotheses of the final
  theorem state this for \hl{the program's dependency closure}: a whole Lean environment is not
  expressible, since \code{Init} holds \code{unsafe} definitions and nested inductives.
\item \lead{Handled by the eraser} the dependency closure passes the router's fragment test
  \code{collectDeps view cfg e}, a decidable, syntactic test that the shipping router itself runs.
  The hypothesis \code{hfrag} of the final theorem states this; the lemma
  \code{collectDeps\_ok\_of} bounds it from below, so that it cannot make the theorem empty.
\item \lead{Everything else} \hl{\code{\#erase} routes it to the unchanged \code{Meta} path}, which
  the theorem does not cover (Section~\ref{sec:trust-trusted}).
\item \lead{Phases} the statement of the final theorem is the same in both phases; only the rules
  of the source semantics and of the erasure relation, and the fragment test, grow.
\end{itemize}

\section{The fragment test}\label{sec:scope-fragment}

The shipping \code{collectDeps view cfg e} (Definition~\ref{def:Erasure-collectDeps}) rejects a
program when its closure, as the pure traversal reads it, contains one of the classes below
(DV-26). The closure holds the term, the types of its constants, the values of the definitions and
opaques it reads, and the types and constructors of the blocks it registers; it skips the values
of theorems and of recognized \code{casesOn} constants.

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Phase I only} a recursor other than the \code{T.rec} of a recognized \code{T.casesOn},
  and any recursor in a read expression; a quotient constant; a constant with an
  \code{\_unsafe\_rec} companion (structural or well-founded recursion).
\item \lead{Both phases} a string literal; an \code{unsafe}, \code{partial} or
  \code{@[implemented\_by]} constant; a nested or unsafe inductive; an \code{@[extern]}
  constructor under \code{preferAxiom}; under \code{nat := .machine} a \code{Nat} literal,
  \code{Nat.zero}, \code{Nat.succ}, \code{Int.ofNat}, \code{Int.negSucc}, \code{Nat.casesOn} or
  \code{Int.casesOn}; under \code{remove\_irrel\_constr\_args} any inductive type; a
  \code{casesOn} or projection on an inductive whose sort may be both \code{Prop} and not
  (\code{PUnit}); a recognized \code{casesOn} whose constructor has an index field
  (\code{Acc}); an inductive whose sort is \code{Prop} under every assignment but not written
  \code{Prop}; a free variable, metavariable or level metavariable; a constant the view does not
  have.
\item \lead{Name collisions} of kernel names and of $\lambda_\Box$ inductive blocks are an error
  of the pure path, not a reason to route.
\end{itemize}

\section{Edges of the fragment}\label{sec:scope-decisions}

Each row fixes an edge of the fragment or of the trust base; a \code{DV-n} is an entry of
\code{doc/DIVERGENCES.md} (Chapter~\ref{chap:divergences}).

\begin{tabular}{p{0.16\linewidth}p{0.76\linewidth}}
\toprule
Edge & Choice \\
\midrule
Term builders & the bridge uses Metalean's five total $\eta$-term builders, which are trusted;
  check C6 exempts them and what they reach (Section~\ref{sec:trust-trusted}) \\
Index fields & the fragment excludes a \code{casesOn} whose constructor has an index field
  (\code{Acc}, \code{Acc.below}); \code{Eq}, \code{HEq}, \code{And} and \code{True} stay in \\
Recursors & the closure keeps the declarations \code{T.casesOn} and \code{T.rec}, not their
  values, so that the recognizer works; a recursor anywhere else is an exclusion \\
Inherited ids & the divergence register keeps the ids 1 to 22 of the lean4lean verification; an
  id that does not apply has a ``not applicable'' entry, so that no citation dangles \\
DV-23 & under-applied constructor and \code{casesOn} spines evaluate to their
  \hl{$\eta$-expansion}, arguments unevaluated under the new binders, as the eraser's
  \code{withAppEtaToMinArity} emits them; PCUIC's \code{eval} evaluates arguments eagerly \\
DV-24 & theorems have no $\delta$, since Metalean loads them as opaque: a proof may evaluate to an
  atom; \code{casesProof} and \code{projProp} cover \code{match h with | rfl} and \code{h.1}; a
  \code{casesOn} on a lemma of an inductive with proof fields does not evaluate \\
DV-25 & a recognized \code{casesOn} is primitive in the source, without $\delta$; recursors have
  no rule \\
DV-26 & the fragment is an explicit hypothesis, \code{hfrag}, with the exclusion classes above \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{The premise \code{hfrag}} explicit, unlike the lean4lean verification's in-scope
  condition: out-of-fragment inputs go to the \code{Meta} path, about which nothing is proved.
\item \lead{Its lower bound} \code{collectDeps\_ok\_of} (Lemma~\ref{lem:EraseProof-collectDeps_ok_of}):
  every typed program whose read closure is inside Phase I, with no recursor read and no name
  collision, is accepted.
\end{itemize}

\section{Evidence}\label{sec:scope-evidence}

\subsection{Generated eliminators}\label{sec:scope-eliminators}

On Lean \code{v4.35.0-rc2}, measured on the 2961 \code{casesOn} definitions of \code{Init},
\code{Std} and \code{Lean}:

\begin{tabular}{p{0.4\linewidth}rp{0.42\linewidth}}
\toprule
Class & Count & Treatment \\
\midrule
canonical: a saturated \code{T.rec} & 2715 & recognized; a $\lambda_\Box$ \code{case} \\
projection-based: one-constructor, large-eliminating \code{Prop} types (\code{And}, \code{Iff},
  \code{True}, \ldots) & 186 & not recognized; unfolded, and their projections, proofs, erase
  to $\Box$ \\
nested & 56 & out \\
unsafe & 4 & out \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Recognizer} a syntactic check of the canonical shape; on these 2961 definitions it
  agrees with Lean's own classification on every one.
\item \lead{Matchers} 2383 of the 13378 matchers of \code{Init}, \code{Std} and \code{Lean} are
  in Phase I (\code{nat := .peano}); the others reach a recursor, an \code{@[extern]} constructor
  or structural recursion.
\item \lead{Prelude} \code{Bool.and} and \code{Bool.not} are defined by \code{Bool.rec} directly,
  so a program using them is in Phase II.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The corpus}\label{sec:scope-corpus}

The 294 \code{\#erase} commands of the shipping corpus, through the shipping fragment test
(\code{tests/harness/expected/pure-harness.txt}): \hl{223 in the fragment, 68 out}, and 3 rejected
for a name collision. Of the 223 pure-path outputs, 58 hold an inductive block, 3 a $\lambda_\Box$
\code{case} and 9 a projection.

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Recursors in Phase I} \code{Nat.rec} through structural recursion, \code{Bool.rec}
  through \code{Bool.and} and \code{Bool.not}, \code{Eq.rec} through \code{Eq.ndrec},
  \code{False.rec} through \code{False.elim}, \code{T.rec} through sparse matchers: the test meets
  the recursive definition or the recursor first.
\item \lead{Consequence} \hl{Phase I exercises inductive types on few programs}; pattern matching
  in practice reaches recursors, so Phase II is where the fragment grows.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Caveats}
\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Caveat} The eraser may still fail on an in-scope input (fuel, oracle failure, name
  collision): no output, no claim.
\item \lead{Caveat} The fragment test is syntactic and the typing hypotheses semantic; the final
  theorem assumes both.
\item \lead{Caveat} The corpus counts measure the shipping test on the shipping corpus; they are
  not a statement of the proof.
\end{itemize}
