\section{What is proved}\label{sec:trust-proved}

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Proved} the six no-confusion lemmas of Metalean's type formers
  (Section~\ref{sec:model-noconf}): no sort, $\Pi$-type, inductive type or quotient type equals,
  as a type, one with another head.
\item \lead{Planned} every other node of the proof library (Section~\ref{sec:open-planned}): the
  bridge, the source semantics, erasability, $\lambda_\Box$ evaluation, the erasure relation, the
  soundness of the pure path, the two milestones and the final theorem.
\item \lead{Axioms} a proved node depends on \code{propext}, \code{Classical.choice} and
  \code{Quot.sound} only; \hl{no \code{sorryAx} and no axiom of Metalean} is allowed.
\item \lead{Pinned footprints} the test module \leanfile{proof/EraseProof/Test/NoConf.lean} pins
  the axioms of each lemma with \code{\#guard\_msgs}; \code{proof/axioms.expected} lists them, and
  \code{proof/ROOTS.txt} names the planned consumer of each (Section~\ref{sec:open-roots}).
\item \lead{Checks} \code{proof/scripts/check.sh} (Section~\ref{sec:trust-checks}) and
  \code{blueprint/scripts/audit.py}, which measures every node: existence, axiom closure, coverage
  of every declaration of \code{EraseProof}, roots, kinds and links.
\end{itemize}

\section{Trusted, not verified}\label{sec:trust-trusted}

The final theorem (Chapter~\ref{chap:final}) speaks about the pure path of \code{\#erase} on the
kernel term. The following are outside every statement, as MetaRocq trusts its quoting.

\begin{tabular}{p{0.3\linewidth}p{0.62\linewidth}}
\toprule
Trusted part & What it does \\
\midrule
Elaboration & produces the kernel term \code{e} of \code{\#erase t} \\
The environment view & \code{EnvView.ofEnvironment} reads the Lean environment the eraser sees
  (Definition~\ref{def:Erasure-EnvView}) \\
The printer and the file writes & the S-expression printer (Definition~\ref{def:Serialize}), and
  the writes of \code{.ast}, \code{.ast.inlinings} and \code{.mli}
  (Definition~\ref{def:Erasure-eraseElab}) \\
The inlining sidecar & the constants \code{.ast.inlinings} asks Peregrine to inline \\
Peregrine & compiles the emitted program \\
The \code{Meta} path & out-of-fragment inputs keep the traversal with the elaborator's oracle and
  Lean's compiler passes (Definition~\ref{def:Erasure-erase}) \\
Metalean's checkers and translator & \code{Metalean.Checker}, \code{Metalean.Frontend} and
  \code{Metalean.Export} are unverified; the bridge is modelled on the translator, and no
  declaration of the proof outside the tests may reach them, except the term builders of
  decision D1 (Section~\ref{sec:trust-checks}) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Partial correctness} Metalean has no normalization theorem: nothing is claimed when
  the eraser fails or the source does not evaluate.
\item \lead{Kernel terms} the theorem is about the kernel term, not Lean's compiled code; theorems
  and opaques do not unfold in the source.
\item \lead{Stuck evaluations} an evaluation that reaches a computational axiom or an opaque has no
  source value, and the theorem says nothing about it.
\end{itemize}

\section{Metalean's trust base}\label{sec:trust-metalean}

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Pin} the \code{metalean} package of Section~\ref{sec:intro-pins}, never edited.
\item \lead{No sorry} Metalean contains no \code{sorry}; the proof inherits none.
\item \lead{One axiom} \code{Cardinal.exists\_inaccessibles}
  (\leanfile{Metalean/SetTheory/Cardinal/Inaccessible.lean}), the existence of inaccessible
  cardinals for Metalean's set-theoretic model. \hl{No module of this repository imports
  \code{Metalean.SetSemantics} or \code{Metalean.SetTheory}}, so the axiom is not in the audited
  environment, and a node that reached it would lose its \code{\textbackslash leanok}.
\item \lead{Module system} Metalean is built with Lean's module system; every file the proof
  imports exposes its definitions (\code{@[expose] public section}), so the kernel and the proofs
  unfold them.
\item \lead{The import check} \leanfile{LeanToLambdaBox/Metalean.lean} imports Metalean into the
  root package and pins the axioms of \leandecl{Metalean.WHRed.defeq} and
  \leandecl{Metalean.Defeq.uniqTy} to the standard three.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Status} \code{check.sh -{}-no-final} passes; \stOpen{} C12 and the final form of C12b,
  which need the final theorem, and C11, which needs its script \code{proof/scripts/c11.sh}.
\item \lead{Decision D1} C6 exempts \hl{Metalean's total $\eta$-term builders}
  \code{Metalean.Frontend.\{indTerm, ctorTerm, recrTerm, wrapCase, quotTerm\}} and the inductive
  \code{Metalean.Export.QuotKind}, with what they reach: 156 constants, none \code{partial},
  \code{opaque}, \code{unsafe} or \code{@[implemented\_by]}.
\item \lead{Why D1 is trusted} the builders shape \code{TrM} in the hypotheses and, through
  \code{Erases.box}, the conclusion: a wrong builder could change which terms count as erasable.
  They are trusted as Metalean's definitions are, and C12b pins their revision.
\item \lead{Statement pin} \code{proof/spec.pin} is written from the approved statements; a change
  to a pinned definition fails C12b, and an approved change of statement regenerates the pin.
\end{itemize}

\section{The shipping code}\label{sec:trust-shipping}

The eraser is Lean meta-code. Its traversal is total, bounded by fuel, and generic over a backend
monad. \code{\#erase} runs it with the pure backend on programs in the fragment
(Chapter~\ref{chap:pure}), and with the \code{CoreM} backend, which asks the elaborator for types,
on the others. The pure path uses structural copies of \code{Expr.instantiate1},
\code{Expr.liftLooseBVars} and \code{Level.hasMVar}
(\leanfile{LeanToLambdaBox/Erasure/ExprOps.lean}).

\begin{definition}[Erased terms]
  \label{def:LBTerm}
  \lean{LBTerm}
  \leanok
  \srcloc{LeanToLambdaBox/Basic.lean}{93}
  \lead{In short} The $\lambda_\Box$ terms the eraser builds: MetaRocq's
  \code{EAst.term} (\code{erasure/theories/EAst.v}) with \hl{free variables added}.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \code{box}, \code{bvar}, \code{lambda}, \code{letIn}, \code{app}, \code{const},
        \code{construct}, \code{case}, \code{proj}, \code{fix}, \code{prim}: the MetaRocq
        constructors \code{tBox}, \code{tRel}, \code{tLambda}, \code{tLetIn}, \code{tApp},
        \code{tConst}, \code{tConstruct}, \code{tCase}, \code{tProj}, \code{tFix}, \code{tPrim};
  \item \code{fvar}: a Lean free variable, used during erasure and abstracted to \code{bvar}
        before a binder is built;
  \item absent: \code{tVar}, \code{tEvar}, \code{tCoFix}, \code{tLazy}, \code{tForce}.
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Abstraction of a free variable]
  \label{def:abstract}
  \lean{abstract, toBvar}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:LBTerm}
  \srcloc{LeanToLambdaBox/Basic.lean}{149}
  \lead{In short} \code{abstract x t} replaces the free variable \code{x} by the
  bound variable of the binders crossed, \hl{without shifting} other indices; \code{toBvar} is
  its structural recursion.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Kernames]
  \label{def:toKername}
  \lean{toKername, Kername, mutualBlockKername}
  \leanok
  \srcloc{LeanToLambdaBox/Basic.lean}{32}
  \lead{In short} The $\lambda_\Box$ names of constants and of inductive blocks.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \code{toKername}: a Lean name as a MetaRocq kername; not injective (register entry R-3);
  \item \code{mutualBlockKername}: the name of a mutual inductive block, its members' names
        \hl{printed without escapes} and joined, so that the kernel evaluates it (S-26).
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Global declarations]
  \label{def:GlobalDecl}
  \lean{GlobalDecl, ConstantBody, MutualInductiveBody, OneInductiveBody, ConstructorBody,
    ProjectionBody}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:LBTerm}
  \srcloc{LeanToLambdaBox/Basic.lean}{194}
  \lead{In short} The declarations of an erased environment.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \code{constantDecl}: a constant with an optional body (none for an axiom);
  \item \code{inductiveDecl}: a block of mutual inductive types: parameter count, and per type
        its name, \hl{propositional flag}, allowed eliminations, constructors and projections.
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Erased programs]
  \label{def:ASTType}
  \lean{ASTType, Program}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:LBTerm, def:GlobalDecl}
  \srcloc{LeanToLambdaBox/Basic.lean}{202}
  \lead{In short} What \code{\#erase} emits: \code{untyped} of a list of named global
  declarations and an optional main term, the \code{Untyped} program of Peregrine's format.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Erasure configuration]
  \label{def:Erasure-ErasureConfig}
  \lean{Erasure.ErasureConfig, Erasure.Config.Extern, Erasure.Config.Nat}
  \leanok
  \srcloc{LeanToLambdaBox/Erasure.lean}{68}
  \lead{In short} The options of \code{\#erase ... config}.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \code{extern}: an \code{@[extern]} constant becomes an axiom (\code{preferAxiom}, the
        default) or keeps its Lean definition (\code{preferLogical});
  \item \code{nat}: \code{Nat} literals become primitive integers (\code{machine}, the default) or
        constructor terms (\code{peano}); the scope needs \code{peano};
  \item \code{csimp}, \code{remove\_irrel\_constr\_args},
        \code{auto\_inline\_typeclass\_dispatch}: Boolean options.
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Traversal context and names]
  \label{def:Erasure-TravCtx}
  \lean{Erasure.TravCtx, Erasure.Local, Erasure.binderNameOf, Erasure.fixDefName}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Erasure-ErasureConfig}
  \srcloc{LeanToLambdaBox/Erasure.lean}{172}
  \lead{In short} The traversal's reader context, and the $\lambda_\Box$ names of
  binders and fixpoint members.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \code{TravCtx}: a Lean local context \code{lctx}, the same binders as a list
        \code{locals} of \code{Local}s (innermost first), the fixpoint variables in scope, the
        configuration;
  \item \code{binderNameOf n}: \code{n} if it is printable ASCII, anonymous otherwise;
  \item \code{fixDefName n}: the name of the fixpoint member for the constant \code{n}.
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Backends and the erasure monad]
  \label{def:Erasure-EraseT}
  \lean{Erasure.EraseT, Erasure.Backend, Erasure.ErasureState, Erasure.run}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:GlobalDecl, def:Erasure-TravCtx}
  \srcloc{LeanToLambdaBox/Erasure.lean}{244}
  \lead{In short} $\mathrm{EraseT}\ m = $ \code{StateT ErasureState (ReaderT TravCtx
  m)} over \hl{a backend monad \code{m}}.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \code{Backend m}: the environment and type operations the traversal uses (constant
        lookup, fresh variables, instantiation, erasability, type inference, the
        \code{Prop}-former test, the level of a term's sort, \code{casesOn} and constructor
        information, \code{@[extern]} and inline attributes, preprocessing, logging, the
        out-of-fuel error);
  \item the \code{CoreM} instance calls the Lean functions the traversal uses on the \code{Meta}
        path: \code{Environment.find?}, \code{Meta.inferType}, \code{Meta.isPropFormerType},
        \code{Meta.getLevel}, \code{isErasable}, and others;
  \item \code{ErasureState}: the registered inductives and constants, the emitted declarations,
        the constants to inline and their inlined sizes.
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Erasability oracle of the \texorpdfstring{\code{Meta}}{Meta} path]
  \label{def:Erasure-isErasable}
  \lean{Erasure.isErasable}
  \leanok
  \srcloc{LeanToLambdaBox/Erasure.lean}{260}
  \lead{In short} A term is erased to $\Box$ iff its type is a proposition or a
  type former, as \hl{decided by the elaborator}.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \lead{Lean} a \code{MetaM} function calling \code{Meta.inferType}, \code{Meta.isProp} and
        \code{Meta.isTypeFormerType}; the \code{CoreM} backend's \code{isErasable};
  \item \lead{Caveat} these belong to Lean's elaborator (\code{Lean.Meta}), a different program
        from the kernel that Metalean models.
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Preprocessing]
  \label{def:Erasure-prepareErasure}
  \lean{Erasure.prepareErasure}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Erasure-ErasureConfig}
  \srcloc{LeanToLambdaBox/Erasure.lean}{371}
  \lead{In short} The \code{CoreM} backend's \code{prepare}: rewrites the input term
  with passes of Lean's compiler before erasure.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \lead{Steps} unsafe-recursion names replaced, \code{macroInline}, \code{inlineMatchers},
        \code{macroInline} again, then (option \code{csimp}) the \code{@[csimp]} replacements;
  \item \lead{Caveat} the result may be ill-typed when a type depends on a function that
        \code{csimp} replaces (its docstring).
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The eraser]
  \label{def:Erasure-erase}
  \lean{Erasure.erase, Erasure.visitExpr, Erasure.visitCases, Erasure.register_inductive,
    Erasure.travFuel}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:LBTerm, def:abstract, def:toKername, def:ASTType, def:Erasure-EraseT,
    def:Erasure-isErasable, def:Erasure-prepareErasure}
  \srcloc{LeanToLambdaBox/Erasure.lean}{1094}
  \lead{In short} Erases a term and its dependencies to a program by a
  \hl{fuel-bounded, generic traversal}; \code{erase} runs it with the \code{CoreM} backend.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \code{erase e config}: runs the backend's \code{prepare}, then \code{visitExpr travFuel}
        in \code{EraseT CoreM}; returns the program (the emitted declarations and the main term)
        and the constants to inline;
  \item \code{visitExpr}: an erasable term becomes $\Box$; otherwise one case per \code{Expr}
        constructor, through the mutually recursive functions of the traversal
        (\code{visitApp}, \code{visitConst}, \code{visitMutual}, and others);
  \item \code{register\_inductive}: emits a block named by \code{mutualBlockKername},
        \code{propositional} iff the backend's \code{isPropFormerType} holds of the type (S-25);
  \item \code{visitCases}: a $\lambda_\Box$ \code{case}; on a one-constructor inductive whose
        discriminant's sort may be \code{Prop}, \hl{the alternative applied to one $\Box$ per
        field}, without \code{case} (S-27);
  \item \code{travFuel} $= 2^{32}$: every function of the traversal takes fuel and fails with
        the backend's \code{outOfFuel} at zero.
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Printing]
  \label{def:Serialize}
  \lean{Serialize, sexpr, LBTerm.to_sexpr}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:LBTerm, def:ASTType}
  \srcloc{LeanToLambdaBox/Printing.lean}{15}
  \lead{In short} The S-expression printer of programs, in Peregrine's \code{.ast}
  format.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \code{Serialize}: a class with one method, \code{to\_sexpr}, to the S-expressions
        \code{sexpr};
  \item \leandecl{LBTerm.to_sexpr}: a \code{partial def}; it reaches \code{unreachable!} on
        \code{fvar}.
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The command \texorpdfstring{\code{\#erase}}{\#erase}]
  \label{def:Erasure-eraseElab}
  \lean{Erasure.eraseElab, Erasure.erasestx}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:Erasure-eraseEntry, def:Erasure-EnvView, def:Serialize, def:Erasure-ErasureConfig}
  \srcloc{LeanToLambdaBox/Erasure/Command.lean}{18}
  \lead{In short} Elaborates the term, erases it with \hl{\code{eraseEntry} over
  \code{EnvView.ofEnvironment}}, and writes the program, the constants to inline and the
  \code{.mli} signature: to files when \code{to} and \code{mli} are given, to the message log
  otherwise.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item the \code{config} term is evaluated with \code{unsafe Elab.Term.evalTerm};
  \item the \code{.mli} signature comes from the Lean type of the term (\code{gen\_mli});
  \item an error of the pure path stops the command with \code{erasure failed: ...}
        (Definition~\ref{def:Erasure-eraseEntry}).
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\section*{Caveats}
\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Caveat} A shipping node within standard axioms has declarations that exist and use
  standard axioms only. It says nothing about their behaviour.
\item \lead{Caveat} The \code{CoreM} backend runs the elaborator's type inference and Lean's
  compiler passes; they are outside every Lean statement, and only out-of-fragment programs reach
  them.
\item \lead{Caveat} A \code{panic!} or \code{unreachable!} of the eraser returns a default value and
  continues (register entries R-4 and R-5, Chapter~\ref{chap:shipping-changes}).
\end{itemize}
